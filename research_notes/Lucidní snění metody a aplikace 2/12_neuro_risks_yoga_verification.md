## Q1. Neuroscience section ("LD je REM s částečně probuzeným pozorovatelem")

### Takeaway
All neuroscience claims hold. The [PK] items (Filevich 2015, Dodet 2015, Voss 2012) are now confirmed from primary sources. Demirel et al. is published in *J Neurosci* 2025 (DOI 10.1523/JNEUROSCI.2237-24.2025, PMC12079745). One count needs fixing: it is 26 subjects and **44** recordings, not 43. The report should also say that Baird 2018 found **no** structural differences, which tempers the Filevich grey-matter claim.

### Cited Findings
- **Demirel et al., journal version → CONFIRMED and upgraded.** Demirel Ç, Gott J, … Dresler M, "Electrophysiological Correlates of Lucid Dreaming: Sensor and Source Level Signatures", *J Neurosci* 2025 Apr 21;45(20):e2237242025, doi:10.1523/JNEUROSCI.2237-24.2025, PMID 40258661. Received 22 Nov 2024, accepted 23 Mar 2025. — [PMC12079745](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12079745/); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.2237-24.2025)
- **Demirel sample "26 lucidních snílků, 43 záznamů" → CORRECTED to 26 subjects and 44 recordings.** The paper says the sample "consist[s] of 26 subjects (25.0 ± 5.1 years, 20 females)… yielding a sample size of 44 sleep recordings". The data came from 5 labs: Radboud 12, Osnabrück 5, MPI Munich 10, UFRN Natal 2, Stanford 27 recordings, with 12 recordings excluded. The methods say "all 43 subject data" in one place, an internal inconsistency in the paper. The topographic and source analyses used a subset of 19 participants with 59 channels. — [PMC12079745](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12079745/)
- **Demirel findings → CONFIRMED, with additions.**
  - Abstract: "while sensor-level differences between LD and nonlucid REM sleep were minimal… Source-level analyses showed significant beta power (12–30 Hz) reductions in right central and parietal areas, including the temporoparietal junction… functional connectivity in the alpha band (8–12 Hz) increased during LD compared with nonlucid REM sleep. During initial LD eye signaling… gamma1 power (30–36 Hz) increased in right temporo-occipital regions, including the right precuneus… increased interhemispheric and inter-regional gamma1 connectivity."
  - Discussion: the data "support recent findings (Baird et al., 2022) that LD is not characterized by EEG frontal activity in frequencies ∼40 Hz when SP [saccadic potential] artifacts are sensibly attenuated."
  - The report omits the beta reduction at the TPJ; it should add it. — [PMC12079745](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12079745/)
- **The preprint differs from the journal version.** The bioRxiv v2 abstract reported "gamma1 power… increases during LD in left-hemispheric temporal areas", a finding not in the journal abstract. Cite the journal version. — [bioRxiv 10.1101/2024.04.09.588765 via Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/search?query=10.1101%2F2024.04.09.588765)
- **Voss 2009 ("velmi malý vzorek") → CONFIRMED.** "Six student volunteers"; 19-channel EEG; "higher-than-REM activity in the gamma band… peaking around 40 Hz… strongest in the frontal and frontolateral region." — [Voss et al. 2009, Sleep, PMC2737577](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2737577/)
- **Voss 2014 (tACS) → CONFIRMED as described.** "current stimulation in the lower gamma band during REM sleep… induces self-reflective awareness in dreams… synchronous oscillations around 25 and 40 Hz." Nat Neurosci 2014, doi:10.1038/nn.3719. — [PubMed 24816141](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24816141/)
- **Baird 2018 Sci Rep → CONFIRMED, with a missing nuance.**
  - 14 individuals with ≥3 LD/week vs. matched controls with ≤1 LD/year. Frequent lucid dreamers showed "increased resting-state functional connectivity between left aPFC and bilateral angular gyrus, bilateral middle temporal gyrus and right inferior frontal gyrus."
  - But: "**no significant differences in brain structure were observed**." — [PMC6290891](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6290891/)
- **Filevich 2015 [PK] → CONFIRMED.**
  - 69 participants (18–37 y), median-split on trait lucidity (n = 31 per group).
  - "Participants in the high-lucidity group showed greater gray matter volume in the frontopolar cortex (BA9/10)… BA9/10 regions… showed increases in BOLD signal during thought monitoring… more strongly in the high-lucidity group."
  - J Neurosci 2015, doi:10.1523/JNEUROSCI.3342-14.2015. — [PMC6605529](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6605529/)
- **Dresler 2012 → CONFIRMED (n = 1 analysable).** "Four experienced lucid dreamers… one subject had 2 episodes of verified lucid REM sleep of sufficient length to be analyzed by fMRI. During lucid dreaming the bilateral precuneus, cuneus, parietal lobules, and prefrontal and occipito-temporal cortices activated strongly." — [Dresler et al. 2012, Sleep, PMC3369221](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3369221/) (abstract via Europe PMC)
- **Dodet 2015 narcolepsy [PK] → CONFIRMED.**
  - 53 narcolepsy patients vs. 53 controls: "Lucid dreaming was achieved by 77.4% of narcoleptic patients and 49.1% of controls (P < 0.05), with an average of 7.6±11 vs. 0.3±0.8 lucid dreams/month (P < 0.0001)."
  - In the lab, "Seven of 12 narcoleptic (and 0 non-narcoleptic) lucid dreamers achieved lucid REM sleep across… 33 naps."
  - Sleep 2015, doi:10.5665/sleep.4516. — [PMC4335518](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4335518/)
- **Gott 2020 → CONFIRMED.** "While self-assessed numbers of awakenings, polyphasic sleep and physiologically validated wake-REM sleep transitions were associated with lucid dreaming, neither self-assessed sleep quality, nor physiologically validated numbers of awakenings were." Consc Cogn 2020, doi:10.1016/j.concog.2020.102988. — [PubMed 32768920](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32768920/)
- **Voss 2012, age decline at ~16 [PK] → CONFIRMED.** Survey of students aged 6–19: "lucid dreaming is quite pronounced in young children, its incidence rate drops at about age 16 years. Increased lucidity was found in those attending higher level compared with lower level schools." J Sleep Res 2012, doi:10.1111/j.1365-2869.2012.01022.x. — [PubMed 22639960](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22639960/)
- **Galantamine (14/27/42 %, n = 121) → CONFIRMED.** Participants woke "approximately 4.5 hours after lights out" and stayed out of bed ≥30 min. Odds ratios were 2.29 (4 mg) and 4.46 (8 mg). — [LaBerge et al. 2018, PLOS ONE, PMC6082533](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6082533/)

### Inferences
- The report's line "Lidé s vysokou lucidností snů mají více šedé hmoty ve frontopolární kůře" should carry a caveat: Baird 2018, an fMRI/MRI study of genuinely frequent lucid dreamers, found connectivity differences but no structural ones. Structure is therefore not consistently replicated. The 2026 De Pisapia study (see "New findings") gives multivariate grey/white-matter support.
- The bioRxiv and J Neurosci versions differ in their gamma findings. Product copy should cite only the journal claims: precuneus gamma at signalling onset, TPJ beta decrease, and alpha/gamma connectivity.

### Gaps
- I did not obtain the full text of Voss 2012 (paywalled) or Voss 2014. The age-16 claim rests on the abstract.
- The 40 Hz tACS non-replication claimed elsewhere in the report was not re-verified in this pass. A Europe PMC search did not surface the replication paper.

## Q2. Predictors and prevalence (section "Nejsilnějším prediktorem…", predictors table)

### Takeaway
The prevalence figures (Saunders 2016, Mota-Rolim 2013) and the Aviram & Soffer-Dudek numbers are confirmed. The synesthesia study can be upgraded from [S] to a primary source: Matsuda & Matsuda 2026, *Front Psychol*, N = 616. There are two corrections. First, the table's "~2,000 respondents" for nightmares and sleep paralysis is wrong: PMC7093643 has 455 UK respondents. Second, Gerhardt & Baird 2024 effect sizes are small (r_sp = 0.16; R² ≈ 0.10), and only daily meditators differed.

### Cited Findings
- **Saunders 2016 → CONFIRMED.** "34 studies yielded a mean estimate of 55%, 95% C.I. [49%, 62%]… frequent lucid dreaming (one or more lucid dreams in a month)… 25 studies yielded a mean estimate of 23%, 95% C.I. [20%, 25%]." "Frequent" means monthly, so the report is right to reject the "weekly" reading. — [Saunders et al. 2016, Consc Cogn, PubMed 27337287](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27337287/)
- **Mota-Rolim 2013 → CONFIRMED.** "Brazilian sample (n = 3,427; median age = 25 years)… 77% of the subjects experienced LD at least once in life (44% up to 10 episodes ever)… for 48% LD subjectively lasted less than 1 min… LD control was rare (29%)." — [PMC3857923](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3857923/)
- **Schredl & Göritz 2025 (stability over 7 years) → PARTIAL (abstract via index).** "Stability of Lucid Dream Frequency and Lucid Dreaming Skills Over a 7-Year Period", *Imagination, Cognition and Personality* 45(1), 2025, doi:10.1177/02762366251349518. N = 1,267. People who practised LD during the interval reported more LDs. SAGE returned 403, so only an index abstract was seen. — [EBSCO abstract](https://www.ebsco.com/articles/literature-and-writing/26da6b1b-fcf2-5df2-9287-a5cbdb1ba6a0/stability-of-lucid-dream-frequency-and-lucid-dreaming-skills-over-a-7-year-period)
- **Schredl 2025 IJoDR (n = 81, 4-week diary) → PARTIAL (search snippet only; IJoDR unreachable).** Neither reality testing nor autosuggestion significantly increased LD frequency. Prior LD frequency was the main predictor. — [IJoDR 110523](https://journals.ub.uni-heidelberg.de/index.php/IJoDR/article/view/110523)
- **Gerhardt & Baird 2024, N = 635 → CONFIRMED N; effect sizes are small.**
  - "In total, 635 participants completed the online survey."
  - Open monitoring practice correlated with LDs in the past 6 months at r_sp = 0.16, p = 0.037. Focused attention (r_sp = 0.08), nondual (0.06) and similar styles were not significant.
  - "daily frequent meditators experience more lucid dreams than non-frequent meditators. However, weekly frequent meditators did not."
  - The multiple regression on monthly LD frequency (transcendence, decentering, curiosity, meta-awareness, meditation frequency) explained "R² = 0.098, F(6, 263) = 4.780, p < 0.001". Transcendence and meta-awareness were significant predictors.
  - Recall correlated with LD frequency at r_sp = 0.44. — [PMC11120098](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11120098/)
- **The table row's "krátký kurz bez efektu" comes from Baird 2019, not Gerhardt & Baird 2024** (see Q5). Cite both.
- **Aviram & Soffer-Dudek 2018 → CONFIRMED with exact statistics.**
  - "Undergraduate students (N = 187)… 2 months later, a subsample (n = 78) reported psychopathology again, and also completed a dream diary each morning for 14 days."
  - "Whereas LD frequency was unrelated to psychopathology, LD intensity, and positive LD emotions, were inversely associated with several psychopathological symptoms. Use of deliberate induction techniques was positively associated with psychopathology and sleep problems."
  - Over time, induced LD frequency predicted an increase in dissociation (β = 0.26, p < 0.01): depersonalization β = 0.25, amnesia β = 0.22, absorption β = 0.17. It also predicted an increase in schizotypy (β = 0.14, p < 0.05). "such effects did not emerge with the spontaneous LD frequency item." — [PMC5875414](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5875414/)
- **Row "Noční můry, spánková paralýza, halucinace v bdění… (~2 000 respondentů)" → CORRECTED: N = 455.** PMC7093643 is Drinkwater, Denovan & Dagnall 2020, *Front Psychol* 11:471: "UK-based online sample of 455 respondents (110 males, 345 females…), who had all previously experienced lucid dreaming." Its main finding is that the Auditory and Visual Hallucinations subscale (IPO-RT) "significantly positively predicted dissociated experiences related to REM sleep". Positive correlations between sleep and paranormal measures "were weak". — [PMC7093643](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7093643/)
- **Synesthesia 2026 [S, PRE] → CONFIRMED and upgraded to primary.**
  - Matsuda E & Matsuda E, "Exploring the link between synesthesia and lucid dreaming through perceptual presence", *Front Psychol* 2026, doi:10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1733841. This is a cross-sectional survey of 616 adults (Japanese TIPI-J; LuCiD scale).
  - "perceptual synesthesia (Visualized sensation, Spatial Sequence) robustly promoted lucid-dream facets—especially control, and also insight, dissociation, and positive emotion—whereas conceptual synesthesia (Language-Color, OLP) showed negative interactions with Openness and Extraversion, thereby attenuating lucid-dream experiences."
  - Lucid dreaming was positively associated with Openness and Extraversion. — [PMC12979406](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12979406/)
- **Age row (no LD decline with age in adults).** In Baird 2019 (ages 25–66), "Age was negatively correlated with dream recall (r_s = −0.24, p = 0.001), but was not associated with lucid dream frequency (r_s = −0.09, p = 0.25)." — [PMC6490164](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6490164/)

### Inferences
- The "Synestezie" row should say "only perceptual subtypes (visualised sensation, spatial sequence); conceptual subtypes (grapheme–colour, OLP) may attenuate". It should cite Matsuda & Matsuda 2026, a cross-sectional study from Japan, instead of The Debrief.
- Effect sizes linking meditation to LD are small (r ≈ 0.16, R² ≈ 0.10). The table should say "slabá korelace" (weak correlation), not just "korelace".

### Gaps
- I could not open the Schredl & Göritz 2025 full text, so the stability coefficients (r over 7 years) are not verified.
- The claim "Otevřenost… r ≈ 0.1–0.2 [PK]" was not verified. Matsuda 2026 reports positive associations with Openness but in a regression framework.

## Q3. Behaviour change numbers (implementation intentions, habits, gamification, attrition)

### Takeaway
Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006 (d = .65, 94 tests) is confirmed. The "newer meta-analysis with 642 tests" is Sheeran, Listrom & Gollwitzer 2024, *European Review of Social Psychology*, and it reports a **range of d = .27 to .66** across outcomes. It is not "Wang et al. 2021" (that is a separate MCII meta-analysis). The report's guess of "d ≈ 0.3–0.5" falls inside that range but should be replaced with the actual range. The ILDIS "about half completed" claim is wrong: 355 of 843 (42 %) of those who started finished, and only 22 % of the 1,618 who registered. The gamification figures need small corrections.

### Cited Findings
- **Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006 → CONFIRMED.** "Findings from 94 independent tests showed that implementation intentions had a positive effect of medium-to-large magnitude (d = .65) on goal attainment." Adv Exp Soc Psychol 38:69–119, doi:10.1016/S0065-2601(06)38002-1. — [Semantic Scholar abstract](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1016/S0065-2601(06)38002-1?fields=title,abstract,year); [open-access KOPS PDF](https://kops.uni-konstanz.de/bitstreams/d4f710b4-a505-49ef-a831-5b8d7675100b/download); [Konstanz listing](https://www.socmot.uni-konstanz.de/publications/implementation-intentions-and-goal-achievement-meta-analysis-effects-and-processes)
- **Newer meta-analysis "642 testů… d ≈ 0,3–0,5 [PK]" → CORRECTED: d = .27–.66, by outcome type.**
  - Sheeran P, Listrom O, Gollwitzer PM (2024), "The when and how of planning: Meta-analysis of the scope and components of implementation intentions in 642 tests", *Eur Rev Soc Psychol*, doi:10.1080/10463283.2024.2334563.
  - "Across 642 independent tests, forming implementation intentions proved effective for cognitive, affective, and behavioural outcomes (.27 ≤ d ≤ .66). Effect sizes were larger when plans had a contingent (if-then) format, participants were highly motivated to pursue the goal, and plans were rehearsed." — [Semantic Scholar record](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1080/10463283.2024.2334563?fields=title,abstract,year,venue); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/10463283.2024.2334563)
- **Lally habit study (median ~66 days, range 18–254) [S] → PARTIAL; the range is confirmed from the primary abstract.**
  - Abstract: "96 volunteers… for 12 weeks… The majority (82) provided sufficient data… The model fitted for 62 individuals, of whom 39 showed a good fit… The time it took participants to reach 95% of their asymptote of automaticity ranged from 18 to 254 days… Missing one opportunity to perform the behaviour did not materially affect the habit formation process."
  - The 66-day figure is in the body text and UCL's press release (not fetched; 403). It applies only to the 39 good-fit participants, and anything beyond 84 days is extrapolated.
  - Published online 2009; print EJSP 40(6):998–1009 (2010), doi:10.1002/ejsp.674. — [Crossref/Wiley DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/ejsp.674)
- **"Missing one opportunity does not matter."** This is directly relevant to the report's design advice (no punishment for broken streaks) and is backed by the Lally abstract quoted above. — [Lally et al.](https://doi.org/10.1002/ejsp.674)
- **Gamification in depression apps (JMIR Ment Health 2021) → CONFIRMED with a correction.**
  - Six et al. 2021: 38 studies (n = 8,110), 50 comparisons. Apps vs. controls: g = −0.27.
  - "The gamification moderator was not a significant predictor of depressive symptoms (**β=–0.03**, SE=0.03; P=.38)… did not show an effect of gamification elements on intervention adherence (**β=–1.93**, SE=2.28; P=.40)."
  - The report attaches β = −0.03 to both outcomes and adherence. The adherence coefficient is −1.93 (n.s.). — [PMC8669581](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8669581/)
- **Gamification for physical activity, g = 0.32 (JMIR Serious Games 2025) → CONFIRMED, but the population must be specified.**
  - Yu et al. 2025 covers **patients with cardiovascular disease**: 6 RCTs, with 5 in the short-term meta-analysis.
  - "small effect… on short-term PA (after sensitivity analysis: Hedges g=0.32, 95% CI 0.19–0.45)". Maintenance effect g = 0.20 at ~2.5 months. "Feedback" was the most important game element.
  - This does not generalise to healthy app users. — [PMC11806271](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11806271/)
- **ILDIS completion "zhruba polovina [PK]" → CORRECTED.**
  - "An initial sample of 1618 participants completed the pre-test questionnaire. A total of 843 participants continued to complete Week 1 of the study and 355 participants completed Week 2."
  - That is 42 % of Week-1 starters and 22 % of registrants. The authors list "the high attrition rate" as a limitation. — [Aspy 2020 ILDIS, PMC7379166](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7379166/)
- **ILDIS WBTB timing.** Participants were "instructed to set an alarm 5 h after going to bed, to place the alarm somewhere that would require getting out of bed." — [PMC7379166](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7379166/)

### Inferences
- For the app: about 58 % of motivated volunteers dropped out within one week of active practice in ILDIS, even before the second week. That supports the report's emphasis on process metrics and a short, low-burden onboarding.
- The 642-test meta-analysis says if-then format, high motivation and rehearsal increase effects. That maps directly onto the MILD rehearsal step.

### Gaps
- I could not fetch the full text of Lally 2010 to quote the 66-day median verbatim.
- The EJSP "less is more" paper (multiple implementation intentions) was not re-checked; the report's link is a webvpn proxy URL, which should be replaced with a DOI.

## Q4. Esoteric methods (section "Ezoterické školy…")

### Takeaway
The binaural meta-analysis numbers and the Weiler 2025 conclusion are confirmed. The Raduga 2021 basics (449 participants, 2 nights, 484 "successful attempts") are confirmed from snippets; the 142 LD figure was not re-verified. **The report's limitation "numerical results of the 1984 hypnosis study not found" can now be partly resolved.** The Lucidity Letter summary shows that every condition except original waking instructions produced significantly more lucid dreamers among 30 formerly non-lucid, highly hypnotisable women, and post-hypnotic suggestion was not superior in the number of successful subjects.

### Cited Findings
- **Garcia-Argibay 2019 → CONFIRMED.** "Twenty-two studies… 35 effect sizes… overall medium, significant, consistent effect size (g = 0.45)… does not seem to be necessary to mask binaural beats with white noise or pink noise… exposure before, and before and during the task produces superior results… Time under exposure contributed significantly." Outcomes: memory, attention, anxiety, analgesia; no LD outcome. *Psychol Res* 83:357–372. — [PubMed 30073406](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30073406/)
- **Dane & Van de Castle 1984, hypnosis → NOW FOUND (partial numbers).** Source: Lucidity Letter summary of Dane's dissertation, reprinted in vol. 10(1&2), 1991.
  - "Subjects were 15 pairs of matched hypnotically susceptible females (ages 18–32) reporting recall of at least one dream a month and no prior experience with lucid [dreaming]". One lab night.
  - Four conditions: A = post-hypnotic suggestion (PHS) + original waking instructions (OWI), N = 8; B = PHS + revised waking instructions (RWI), N = 7; C = OWI only, N = 8; D = RWI only, N = 7.
  - Result: "When compared to the effects of Original Waking Instructions (Condition C)… each of the three other conditions were independently significant at the .05 level or better… No statistically significant difference was found between any pairwise combination of these three techniques. That is, PHS, RWI and their combination were all equally effective with respect to the number of subjects who reported lucid dreams."
  - Counts in the text: in condition B "all of the seven successful subjects"; in condition D "four of the six successful subjects" (so 6/7 succeeded). Condition C "was not successful".
  - Qualitative edge for PHS: "6 of the 14 REM-associated lucid dreams in two PHS conditions… occurred between 3 a.m. and 7 a.m.", whereas all non-PHS REM lucidity occurred before 3 a.m. — [Lucidity Letter PDF](https://journals.macewan.ca/lucidity/article/download/444/346); [article page](https://journals.macewan.ca/lucidity/article/view/444)
- **Raduga, IJoDR 2021 → PARTIAL.** "An effective lucid dreaming method by inducing hypnopompic hallucinations", IJoDR 14(1):1–9. Data from 449 mostly beginner participants over two nights, with "484 successful attempts". The method is described as derived from Tibetan dream yoga and was tested at commercial events. I could not re-verify the "142 LD (29 %)" figure or the denominator because the host was unreachable. — [IJoDR 71170 (search snippet)](https://journals.ub.uni-heidelberg.de/index.php/IJoDR/article/view/71170)
- **Weiler, Moreira-Almeida & Monti 2025 → CONFIRMED.** "We conclude that neither the previously proposed features nor the interpretation of the experience can reliably differentiate them. We suggest that disembodiment, or existing without a physical body, should be the key phenomenological feature of OBEs." They also propose focusing on "the immediate and unfiltered sensory experience… rather than the cognitive interpretation." *Front Psychol* 2025, doi:10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1600707. — [PMC12477129](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12477129/)
- **Hypnosis case literature (new).** A 2026 case study, Ramos, "Relational hypnotherapy, lucid dreaming, and sleep paralysis", *Am J Clin Hypn*, doi:10.1080/00029157.2026.2661649. It is a single case only; I found no RCT. — [Europe PMC search result, PMID 42090728](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/42090728)

### Inferences
- The Dane study supports the report's position that self-hypnosis is a legitimate way to deliver a MILD-style intention, but not something superior: waking instructions revised to include sleep-onset feedback (RWI) worked as well. The sample was small (N = 30 in total, highly hypnotisable women only) and the lab procedure included verbal feedback across awakenings, so it does not transfer cleanly to home use.

### Gaps
- Table 2 of Dane (per-condition counts for A and C) is an image and was not in the extracted text.
- Raduga 2021 full text: unreachable host.

## Q5. Tibetan dream yoga (section "Tibetská jóga snů a spánku…")

### Takeaway
Baird 2019 (38 vs. 140; MBSR null) and the 2025 *Dreaming* review (3 studies / 6 substudies, inconclusive) are confirmed. Wolk 2024 (2/5 signal-verified) is confirmed. **Morris 2026 needs correcting.** Neither of its two LDs was signal-verified. Only one happened in the lab, and the monk's first LD occurred at home the night after his lab session. Torres Platas: 21 lucid episodes, of which 7 were PSG-verified. On ClinicalTrials.gov, NCT07217340 is now **completed** (n = 25, no results posted), NCT07408206 is **recruiting** (target 70), and NCT07609537 is a completed narcolepsy RCT (n = 98) from Colombia that is not dream-yoga related. The Wangyal night-session and gender-posture details remain **unverified**.

### Cited Findings
- **Baird 2019 → CONFIRMED with exact figures.**
  - "long-term meditators (LTMs; N=38) and meditation-naïve participants (MNP; N=140)"; ages 25–66.
  - Long-term meditators: median 10 years (range 2–35), 2,639 total hours, median 5.9 h/week. They "reported a higher frequency of lucid dreams (M=1.11 per month, SD=2.28) compared to MNPs (M=0.45 per month, SD=1.22) [p=0.01]."
  - RCT arm: meditation-naïve participants were randomised to 9-week MBSR, the Health Enhancement Program (HEP, active control) or waitlist, with follow-up at about 6 months. "neither MBSR nor HEP resulted in increases in reported lucid dream frequency post-intervention (T2) or at long-term follow-up (T3)."
  - Meditators with frequent LD came from mixed traditions (4 Theravada, 3 Tibetan, 2 Zen, 1 Vipassana), with no difference between traditions. *Psychol Conscious* 6(1):40–54, doi:10.1037/cns0000176.
  - The earlier notes' "≥5 years, ≥200 min/week" is not how the paper describes the sample; use the medians above. — [PMC6490164](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6490164/)
- **2025 *Dreaming* systematic review → CONFIRMED.** Balt AC, Nieuwenhuis M, de Bruin EJ (2025), "The Relationship Between Mindfulness and Lucid Dreaming: A Systematic Review", *Dreaming*, doi:10.1037/drm0000303. "A total of 555 unique studies were screened, of which three studies (consisting of a total of six substudies) were included… narrative synthesis. The findings reveal an inconclusive association between mindfulness and LDF." Candidate moderators: meditation experience, LD experience, dream recall, gender, age. — [U Twente record](https://research.utwente.nl/en/publications/the-relationship-between-mindfulness-and-lucid-dreaming-a-systema/)
- **Wolk et al. 2024 → CONFIRMED.** IJoDR 17(2):224ff (2024), doi:10.11588/ijodr.2024.2.98233. Five participants; WBTB after about 5 h; a breath-counting mindfulness task paired with music cues during waking; cues replayed in REM. - **Morris et al. 2026 "2 z 5 s LD, včetně mnicha" → CORRECTED (needs nuance).**

The file name suggests a *Psychological Science* revision; status unconfirmed. — [Northwestern-hosted PDF](https://bpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/sites.northwestern.edu/dist/4/2492/files/2026/07/DYX_PsychSci_R17_unblinded.pdf)

### Inferences
- The report's summary sentence "laboratorní ověření je zatím na úrovni pilotů s 5–6 účastníky" is still accurate. The evidence ladder is: Wolk (2/5 signal-verified), Morris (0 signal-verified, 2 LD reports), Torres Platas (7 PSG-verified lucid episodes in 6 trained experts). The Morris result should not be cited as a lab success for the monk.
- The first controlled dream-yoga trial (NCT07217340) has finished, but it is a 25-person feasibility study with retention and acceptability as primary outcomes. Even when published it will not test efficacy. NCT07408206 measures theta and sleep architecture, not LD or anxiety outcomes, as primary.
- NCT07609537 is a new, relevant clinical item for the "therapy/risks" section (LD training for narcolepsy), not for dream yoga. Its retrospective registration lowers confidence.

### Gaps
- Wangyal's night-session protocol and gender posture rule remain [NEOV]. I chose not to use a full-text PDF circulating on a third-party site because it appeared to be an unauthorised copy. A legitimate check needs the physical book (Snow Lion 1998 / Shambhala 2022 revision) or Ligmincha course materials.
- Namkhai Norbu's "A" placement (centre of body vs. throat) was not re-checked.

## Q6. Risks, benefits and safety ("Rizika…", "Další užitečné poznatky")

### Takeaway
The PTSD trial is real: Yount et al. 2025, *European Journal of Trauma & Dissociation* 9(1):100510, n = 49 vs. 50 waitlist, 22 h over 6 days, preregistered. Effects were moderate to large (d = 0.57 at day 6, d = 0.81 at day 28 for PCL-5), but about half of the *control* group also had LDs, and LD did not correlate with improvement. Stumbrys 2023, Mallett 2022 and Spoormaker 2006 are confirmed. Holzinger 2020 found that lucid dreaming therapy (LDT) did not improve sleep variables. Soffer-Dudek 2020 and Vallat & Ruby 2019 are opinion and perspective pieces: they support "fragmentation is a theoretical concern", not "it scales with frequency" as an empirical finding. One citation is mislabelled: the "lucid nightmares / Stumbrys 2018" bullet links to the Drinkwater 2020 paper.

### Cited Findings
- **Yount et al. 2025 PTSD RCT → CONFIRMED and expanded.**
  - "Decreased PTSD symptoms following a lucid dreaming workshop: A randomized controlled study", *Eur J Trauma Dissociation* 9(1):100510, doi:10.1016/j.ejtd.2025.100510. The workshop spanned "22 hours over six consecutive days". "randomly assigned to either an active workshop group (n = 49) or a wait-list control group (n = 50)."
  - "Roughly half of the participants in both the workshop and control groups experienced at least one lucid dream during the workshop period. Among these, 63% of workshop participants versus 38% of controls achieved a healing lucid dream… No significant correlation was found between lucid dreams and reductions in PTSD and nightmare symptoms."
  - PCL-5 change: workshop −22.7 vs. control −8.8 at day 6 (d = .57, 95 % CI .11–1.03); −25.8 vs. −9.6 at day 28 (d = .81, CI .35–1.3).
  - Nightmare distress at day 28: −4.5 vs. −0.5 (d = .78). At day 6 it was not significant (p = .07).
  - Limitations: LD occurrence was self-reported with no PSG; the morning survey wording confused controls about "healing LDs"; there were missing data. Analyses were preregistered (OSF zu89w), with FDR correction.
  - Funded by the John Sperling Foundation; affiliation is the Institute of Noetic Sciences (IONS). The authors declare no conflict of interest. — [VU repository PDF](https://epublications.vu.lt/object/elaba:225950437/225950437.pdf); [IONS summary](https://noetic.org/publication/decreased-ptsd-symptoms-following-a-lucid-dreaming-workshop-a-randomized-controlled-study/)
- **Earlier pilot (context).** Yount et al., *Traumatology* (2024), doi:10.1037/trm0000456: a within-participant design, 49 adults, 6-day online workshop. Of these, 37 achieved ≥1 LD and 25 enacted a healing plan. — [PsyArXiv/Crossref record](https://doi.org/10.31234/osf.io/wr4mx)
- **Spoormaker & van den Bout 2006 → CONFIRMED.** "23 nightmare sufferers were randomly divided into 3 groups; 8… one 2-hour individual LDT session, 8… one 2-hour group LDT session, and 7… waiting list… At follow-up the nightmare frequency of both treatment groups had decreased. There were no significant changes in sleep quality and posttraumatic stress disorder symptom severity. **Lucidity was not necessary for a reduction in nightmare frequency.**" *Psychother Psychosom* 75:389–394. — [PubMed 17053341](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17053341/)
- **Holzinger, Saletu & Klösch 2020 → CONFIRMED (new to the report).** PTSD patients randomised to LDT (n = 20) or an active comparison (n = 11). "LDT had no effect on the investigated sleep variables. No correlation between reduction of nightmare severity and changes in PTSD-profile (IE-S) was found. Nevertheless, levels of anxiety and depression decreased significantly." *Front Psychol* 11:1826. — [PMC7471655](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7471655/)
- **Tzioridou et al. 2025 → CONFIRMED as a narrative review.** It covers nightmare disorder, depression, anxiety, psychosis, dissociative states, dream/sleep yoga and yoga nidra, plus "potential drawbacks of lucid dreaming interventions". *Neurosci Biobehav Rev* 2025, doi:10.1016/j.neubiorev.2025.106011, PMID 39818345. The co-author list includes Soffer-Dudek, Stumbrys, Torres-Platas, Gott and Dresler. I saw only the abstract, so the "LDT does not beat IRT" conclusion was not verified from this source. — [PubMed 39818345](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39818345/)
- **Soffer-Dudek 2020 → CONFIRMED, but it is an opinion piece.** "Are Lucid Dreams Good for Us? Are We Asking the Right Question? A Call for Caution in Lucid Dream Research", *Front Neurosci*. It argues frequent induction may harm mental health via "(1) possibly disrupted sleep quality and (2) possibly disrupted reality–fantasy boundaries" and that "techniques such as reality testing… require… disruption of sleep hygiene by deliberate confusion of the sleep and waking states." — [PMC6993576](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6993576/)
- **Vallat & Ruby 2019 → CONFIRMED (perspective piece).** Induction methods based on forced awakenings "disturb sleep by increasing its fragmentation, modifying its architecture and decreasing its duration". *Front Psychol* 10:2585. — [PMC6874013](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6874013/)
- **Mallett et al. → CONFIRMED.** "Benefits and concerns of seeking and experiencing lucid dreams: benefits are tied to successful induction and dream control", *Sleep Advances* (OUP, 2022), doi:10.1093/sleepadvances/zpac027. A content analysis of 400 r/LucidDreaming posts (the subreddit had ~419,000 members on 28 Oct 2021). "Negative experiences, such as sleep paralysis and restlessness, were associated with failed lucid-dreaming attempts rather than lucid dreaming itself"; "successfully inducing high-control lucid dreams poses low risk." — [PMC10104404](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10104404/)
- **Stumbrys 2023 [S] → CONFIRMED (press summary consistent with journal listing).**
  - *Psychology of Consciousness* 10(2):152–163, doi:10.1037/cns0000288. Online survey, N = 489, mostly US; measures were PSQI, Multiscale Dissociation Inventory, SWEMWBS and UCLA Loneliness.
  - "only about 10% considered to be emotionally negative… frequency of lucid dreams was not associated with total sleep quality scores or with dissociation."
  - However, higher LD frequency "was associated with more sleep disturbances but also a greater subjective sleep quality… linked to higher levels of derealization but fewer memory disturbances." The proportion of deliberately induced LDs was associated with higher well-being. — [PsyPost](https://www.psypost.org/can-lucid-dreaming-be-dangerous-new-research-suggests-lucid-dreaming-is-generally-a-safe-and-positive-experience/)
- **ILDIS "induction did not worsen sleep quality" → CONFIRMED, with the authors' caveat.** "The use of self-report measures is a potential limitation to the findings that lucid dream induction did not adversely affect sleep quality." — [PMC7379166](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7379166/)
- **Citation mismatch in the "Lucidní noční můry" bullet → CORRECTED.** The bullet names "Stumbrys 2018, Dreaming" but links to `frontiersin.org/…fpsyg.2020.00471`, which is Drinkwater, Denovan & Dagnall 2020 (lucid dreaming, nightmares, sleep paralysis and paranormal belief; N = 455). A related newer primary source on lucid nightmares is Löhner, Schredl & Göritz (2025), "Lucid nightmares and lucid nightmare distress: Associated factors and coping strategies", *Dreaming*, doi:10.1037/drm0000316 (title only seen). — [PMC7093643](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7093643/); [Crossref DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/drm0000316)
- **Konkoly 2021 two-way communication, "~18 %" [V/PK] → CONFIRMED as 18.4 %.** "we observed a correct response on 18.4% of these trials." There were 29 correct answers from 6 of 36 participants, out of 158 question occasions during signal-verified LDs. — [PMC8162929](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8162929/)
- **Stumbrys 2016 motor practice [PK] → CONFIRMED.** "An online experiment was completed by 68 participants within four groups… All three practice groups significantly improved… no significant improvements… for the control group. Subjective sleep quality was not affected by night practice." Finger-tapping; J Sports Sci, doi:10.1080/02640414.2015.1030342. — [PubMed 25846062](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25846062/)
- **Erlacher 2014 ("~1.5× longer") [PK] → PARTIAL: true for walking only.** Durations in LD were "for counting… 27.2%, for walking 52.5% and for the gymnastic routine 23.2% longer than in wakefulness". Earlier work found squats took ~40 % longer. Samples were small (e.g., five proficient lucid dreamers in condition 1). *Front Psychol* 4:1013. — [PMC3893623](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3893623/)

### Inferences
- The report's line "Výskyt LD ale nekoreloval se zlepšením" is correct and should be strengthened. Yount 2025, Spoormaker 2006 and Holzinger 2020 all show symptom gains that do not track lucidity, so the active ingredient is probably exposure, mastery and imagery (IRT-like). This supports the report's choice of an IRT-style nightmare module over "lucidity as therapy".
- Because roughly half of waitlist controls also had LDs, LD frequency is not a good efficacy metric even in a trial. The app should measure symptom and distress change separately from LD count.
- Stumbrys 2023 shows a signal of more sleep disturbances and more derealization alongside the benign averages. Keeping the derealization and depersonalization check-in (design item 4) is justified, not over-cautious.
- Soffer-Dudek 2020 and Vallat & Ruby 2019 should be cited as "expert caution and theoretical risk". The report's wording "škálují s frekvencí" (scale with frequency) implies a dose-response finding that neither paper provides.

### Gaps
- No full text of Tzioridou 2025 (Elsevier), so the IRT comparison was not checked there; PMC7900550 was downloaded but not re-checked for this.
- I found no data on adverse events in commercial LD apps, which matches the report's limitation.
- The [PK] risk claims (bipolar: sleep loss triggers mania; WBTB vs. CBT-I; photosensitive epilepsy) were not checked in this pass; they are general clinical knowledge outside the LD literature.

## New findings worth adding

### Takeaway
Five items would strengthen the report: (1) the J Neurosci version of Demirel, which shows a TPJ beta decrease and no frontal 40 Hz signal; (2) De Pisapia 2026, a structural MRI study; (3) the full Yount 2025 RCT effect sizes and the caveat that controls also had LDs; (4) the narcolepsy LD-training RCT NCT07609537; (5) Sheeran 2024's moderators for implementation intentions (if-then format, high motivation, rehearsal).

### Cited Findings
- **De Pisapia et al. 2026, *J Sleep Res*** ("Lucid Dreaming Frequency Associated With Grey-White Matter Networks: An Exploratory Multimodal MRI Study", doi:10.1111/jsr.70305).
  - "lucid dreaming frequency was associated with one joint GM-WM component, encompassing frontal, temporal, parietal, and cerebellar regions implicated in metacognition, imagery, and volitional control, as well as one GM-specific component involving visual and attentional areas including the cuneus. In contrast, ordinary dream recall frequency was associated exclusively with two WM-specific components, showing no overlap."
  - This is exploratory work using mCCA + jICA. — [PMC13357955](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13357955/)
- **Demirel 2025 adds a TPJ beta decrease** during LD vs. nonlucid REM, interpreted as reassessment of the reality of perception. It also states frontal ~40 Hz is not a signature of LD once saccade artefacts are removed. This directly undercuts "gamma/40 Hz = lucidity" marketing. — [PMC12079745](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12079745/)
- **Narcolepsy LD-training RCT (NCT07609537)**: 98 NT1 patients, six 90-min telehealth CBT-NAR sessions with LD and imagery control vs. standard care. Completed, no results posted, registered retrospectively. Relevant to the report's "Narkolepsie" row, which currently says only "under supervision, no WILD". — [ClinicalTrials.gov](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07609537)
- **Torres Platas: signal-detection rate.** In expert dream-yoga practitioners only 15 of 20 self-reported signals (75 %) were detectable in PSG, and 7 of 11 REM lucid episodes were verified. This is a useful benchmark for consumer devices that claim to "detect" lucidity. — [PDF](https://bpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/sites.northwestern.edu/dist/4/2492/files/2026/07/DYX_PsychSci_R17_unblinded.pdf)
- **Implementation-intention moderators (Sheeran et al. 2024).** "Effect sizes were larger when plans had a contingent (if-then) format, participants were highly motivated to pursue the goal, and plans were rehearsed." These are concrete MILD script design rules. — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/10463283.2024.2334563)
- **Dane 1984 timing observation.** Post-hypnotic suggestion produced 6 of 14 REM lucid dreams in late REM periods (3–7 a.m.), whereas all non-hypnosis REM lucidity occurred before 3 a.m. This is a hint that pre-sleep suggestion can carry into late-night REM. — [Lucidity Letter PDF](https://journals.macewan.ca/lucidity/article/download/444/346)
- **Lally: one missed day "did not materially affect the habit formation process."** This is direct evidence for the report's design advice against streak penalties. — [Lally et al.](https://doi.org/10.1002/ejsp.674)

### Inferences
- With these corrections the report's broad conclusions stand: timing and training matter more than hardware, LD is a metacognitive state rather than a "frequency", and risk lies in fragmentation and failed attempts. Only specific numbers and citation labels need changing.

### Gaps
- IJoDR remained unreachable, which blocked Raduga 2021, Schredl 2025 and the alien-encounter study, and stopped me checking the 142 LD / 29 % and 75 % figures from full text.
- The Wangyal protocol details still need the physical book.
