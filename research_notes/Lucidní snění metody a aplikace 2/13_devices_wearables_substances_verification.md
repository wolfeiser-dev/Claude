## 1. Devices: status, price, shipping, evidence (Oct 2026)

### Takeaway
The report's general picture holds: LD hardware is a graveyard, and nothing has independent efficacy data. Several numbers and statuses need updating, though.
- ZMax costs **€1,200** (LD set, excl. VAT), not ~US$900.
- Remee is now **US$67** shipped.
- Prophetic **Phase uses fNIRS, not EEG**, and the Dual has no neural sensors. Batch 1 of both is "sold out" and batch 2 ships early or mid-2027.
- LucidMe is at best uncertain to obtain.
- New entrants exist: the INSPEC (bedside IR camera, €300) and the Somni Mask Pro (Kickstarter Sep–Oct 2026).
- Several "sleep neurotech" headbands (Elemind, Somnee, Muse S Athena, Neurable) have no LD function. Elemind does not ship to the EU at all.

### Inferences
- For a Czech/EU user, the only things orderable today with any REM sensing and an LD focus are the ZMax (research tool, about €1,450 with VAT and shipping; my calculation at 21% Czech VAT) and the INSPEC (€300, vendor-only validation). Remee ships from the US with no sensing. Prophetic is US-based pre-order. Elemind cannot be bought in the EU.
- Prophetic's own "evidence" chart has no n, no blinding and no protocol, and uses 1–10 self-ratings, so it is marketing material rather than data. The Dual has no sensor at all, so it cannot time stimulation to REM by measurement. (The product page says nothing about how timing works. This is an inference from the published spec, which lists no neural sensors for the Dual.)
- The INSPEC comparison page is the best current market overview, but it is written by a competitor. All its statements about rivals (LucidMe, Somni, ELI) should be presented as "according to a competitor".

## 2. Wearable sleep staging vs PSG, and phone-only REM detection

### Takeaway
All three figures in the report are correct, but each needs context.
- Oura study: the four-stage kappas 0.65/0.60/0.55 come from **one night, n=35 healthy adults, funded by Oura**.
- Lee 2023: an independent-ish but SleepRoutine-affiliated multicentre study, which gave **much lower kappas for the same devices** (Oura 3 κ≈0.35, Apple Watch 8 κ≈0.30).
- Apple's Oct 2025 white paper: REM correctly classified **78% (2022 algorithm) → 82% (watchOS 26 algorithm)**, median four-stage κ 0.63 → 0.68. Apple publishes no REM precision.
- Per-device REM sensitivity across studies: about 33% (Garmin Vivosmart 4) to about 83% (Apple Watch 8, in the Oura-funded study).
- Phone-only accelerometer apps are poor for REM. Sleep as Android's own "50% of cues land in REM" figure is real, but it now sits on its sleep-lab-comparison page, not the LD docs page.

### Inferences
- When the report quotes kappas, it should add that **study design changes rankings dramatically**. Oura and Apple score about 0.6 in the Oura-funded single-night study but 0.30–0.35 in the larger Korean multicentre study (which itself has a SleepRoutine conflict). A safe summary: "four-stage κ roughly 0.2–0.7 depending on device and study; REM sensitivity about 33–83%".
- For an LD app, the relevant number is REM **precision** (the share of "REM" calls that really are REM), because a cue fired in wake or light sleep is wasted or wakes the user. Only the Oura-funded study reports it (73–79%). Apple reports only recall.
- None of these accuracy figures apply in real time, since HealthKit and Health Connect stages are available only after the session (unchanged from the report).
- Rough estimate (inference): a phone/actigraphy-only REM guess gives about a 50% hit rate. A good watch classifier run offline gives about 60–80% REM recall. A forehead EEG (ZMax, Waveband) approaches PSG.

### Gaps
- Galaxy Watch 3 kappa and REM precision were not extracted. No Samsung validation of the current Galaxy Watch (7/8/Ultra) sleep-stage algorithm was found.
- No validation of Pixel Watch 3/4 or a Google white paper was found.
- No validation of a phone accelerometer placed on or under the mattress for REM was found. Ding et al. 2022 (under-mattress Sleeptracker-AI, n=102) is by the manufacturer; REM numbers were not obtained.
- The kappa type in Schyvens 2025 (two-state vs four-state) is ambiguous in the paper.

## 3. Neurostimulation (tACS, tDCS, tFUS)

### Takeaway
The report's statements hold.
- Voss 2014: n=27 non-lucid dreamers; 30 s of frontotemporal tACS 2 min into REM; 2–100 Hz; lucidity rose only at 25 and 40 Hz. The often-quoted "77%" comes from press coverage, not the abstract.
- Blanchette-Carrière 2020 (Nielsen lab) found signal-verified LDs with tACS **but equally with sham**. The abstract does not give n, so the report's "n = 40" is secondary.
- Stumbrys 2013 tDCS: weak effect, only in frequent lucid dreamers.
- No 2024–2026 tACS/tDCS LD replication was found.
- New in 2026: vestibular stimulation (GVS) in REM was associated with higher lucidity ratings (Peters et al., Sleep Advances 2026, n=28).
- New in 2026: a tFUS sleep wearable (NEUSLeeP, UT Austin, Nature Communications 2026) shortened REM latency, but measured no LD outcomes.

### Inferences
- The Blanchette-Carrière result ("sham also produced SVLDs") is a stronger point than "no effect". It shows that the lab setting, expectation and procedure alone induce LD, which fits the report's thesis that training and context do the work.
- GVS (galvanic vestibular stimulation) is a new candidate cue modality, but it is electrical, so it is not app-implementable and the sample is small. It is relevant only as a research trend.
- tFUS can measurably modulate REM timing in humans (NEUSLeeP), which makes Prophetic's mechanism less fanciful. It still says nothing about lucidity, and Prophetic targets the prefrontal cortex, not the subthalamic nucleus.

### Gaps
- Exact n, design and SVLD counts of Blanchette-Carrière 2020: full text inaccessible (ScienceDirect 403).
- Voss 2014 original per-frequency rates were not checked against the paper (press only). The DOI and title of the NEUSLeeP Nature Communications paper were not obtained.
- No safety data on tFUS during sleep for consumer devices were found.

## 4. Substances (with Czech/EU legal status)

### Takeaway
- Galantamine figures are correct (n=121; 14/27/42% **of participants** reporting at least one LD on that condition night; mild side effects 12% overall vs 3% placebo).
- In the CZ, every registered galantamine product (Reminyl and generics) is **"na lékařský předpis" (Rx) per SÚKL**.

- The donepezil "8 of 10" figure comes from **LaBerge's 2004 patent**, not a peer-reviewed study.
- B6: Aspy 2018 was itself a replication of Ebben 2002, and no 2024–2026 replication was found.

- No new 2024–2026 pharmacological LD trial was found.

### Inferences
- In Czech terms: galantamine is a **prescription-only medicine** (all SÚKL products Rx; ATC N06DA04). Foreign "galantamine supplements" ordered online are, at best, mislabelled. In EU law a product containing a pharmacologically active substance like this would most likely be an unauthorised medicinal product. That is my inference and has not been checked against SÚKL or SZPI statements. The app should not mention online sources.
- Calea, Silene and mugwort are **legal to possess in the CZ** (not on NV 463/2013 or NV 456/2025). Legality is not the same as safety or marketability: selling them as food or supplements falls under food law (novel food etc.; not checked). Calea has in vitro neuroglial-toxicity signals. Poland treats Calea as a narcotic (group I-N), which matters for EU users who travel or order across borders.
- The **psychomodulační látky** framework could in future absorb "legal highs" (MZ can add substances by government decree). At present, Oct 2026, it covers only kratom.
- Evidence grading in the report's credibility table: donepezil should move from "Malé studie n≈10" to "patent data (LaBerge 2004), no peer-reviewed trial". The galantamine row could add "doplňky z internetu: 9/10 chybně označených (JAMA 2024)".
