# Technology, sensory stimulation, neurostimulation, pharmacology and hardware for lucid dreaming (LD) induction — state as of Oct 2026

Research method note: in this session full-text fetching (WebFetch/curl) was blocked by the network proxy for all sites, so the findings below come from search-engine summaries of the sources (abstracts, press releases, reviews, developer forums). Numbers came from those summaries and were cross-checked between sources where possible; a few need checking against full texts (listed under Gaps). The web-search budget ran out before the last three queries (phone-only sleep staging accuracy, Dreamento details, Kokoon/Muse S).

Abbreviations: LD = lucid dream; SVLD = signal-verified lucid dream (lucidity confirmed by pre-agreed left-right eye movements on PSG/EEG); TLR = Targeted Lucidity Reactivation; PSG = polysomnography; MILD = Mnemonic Induction of LD; SSILD = Senses-Initiated LD; WBTB = wake back to bed; TDI = Targeted Dream Incubation.

## 1. Sensory cueing during REM (light, sound, vibration, smell), TLR, two-way communication, at-home studies, Dormio

### Takeaway
External cues are the best-supported technology for LD, but only when they are paired with pre-sleep training: the cue has to be linked in advance to a "lucid mindset" (the TLR principle). Results: about 50% SVLD in a lab nap with cues vs 17% without (Carr 2020); in Konkoly et al. 2024, a smartphone-only TLR app (no PSG, no wearable) raised LD frequency from 0.74 to 2.11 per week, and a controlled experiment beat blinded control nights. However, the largest preregistered multicentre study (Esfahani/Dresler, 2024 preprint, revised 2026) found high LD rates overall but no clear benefit of REM cueing over SSILD training alone. That is the most important caveat for an app.

### Cited Findings
**Historical light cues (LaBerge, DreamLight/NovaDreamer)**
- The Lucidity Institute's DreamLight and DreamLink were the first LD masks on the market and sold for over US$1,000. NovaDreamer followed in 1993; one review site says it was discontinued in 2004, and used units sell for about US$300–600 — [Sleep Foundation](https://www.sleepfoundation.org/dreams/lucid-dream-mask); [World of Lucid Dreaming – NovaDreamer review](https://www.world-of-lucid-dreaming.com/novadreamer.html)
- In the early DreamLight study, 11 subjects (78%) reported 32 LD episodes: 22 on nights with light cues and 10 on nights without — [Mota-Rolim et al. 2019, Frontiers in Neuroscience, "Portable Devices to Induce Lucid Dreams—Are They Reliable?"](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6517539/)
- In the mask study the Sleep Foundation summarises, participants were more than twice as likely to have an LD on nights when the mask gave cues. The mask was sometimes switched off, so the effect is more than placebo, but the study is small and old. The Sleep Foundation's conclusion: "research on lucid dream masks is limited… there isn't enough evidence to conclude that they work reliably for everyone"; masks work best combined with MILD — [Sleep Foundation](https://www.sleepfoundation.org/dreams/lucid-dream-mask)
- Stumbrys et al. 2012 classify external stimuli for LD into: light, acoustic, vibro-tactile, electro-tactile, vestibular and water stimulation. The Tan & Fan 2023 review uses a modified scheme with four kinds of external stimulation — [Tan & Fan 2023, J Sleep Res, "A systematic review of new empirical data on lucid dream induction techniques"](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jsr.13786); original review [Stumbrys et al. 2012, Consciousness and Cognition, "Induction of lucid dreams: A systematic review of evidence"](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1053810012001614)
- Tan & Fan 2023: 19 peer-reviewed studies, 14 techniques. MILD was the most effective; SSILD and galantamine are "possible competitors" that need replication. Methodological quality was moderate in 17 studies and poor in 3 (the counts as reported in the summary) — [Tan & Fan 2023](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jsr.13786)

**Targeted Lucidity Reactivation (TLR) — lab**
- Carr, Konkoly, Mallett, Edwards, Appel & Blagrove (2020) published in *Psychology of Consciousness: Theory, Research, and Practice*, vol. 7 (NOT in Consciousness and Cognition): "Combining presleep cognitive training and REM-sleep stimulation in a laboratory morning nap for lucid dream induction". Across two nap times, the protocol (visual and auditory cues paired with pre-sleep training, then replayed during REM) produced SVLDs in 50% of cued participants vs 17% without cueing. Three of the successful participants had never had an LD before — [Swansea Cronfa record](https://cronfa.swan.ac.uk/Record/cronfa53997); [preprint PDF](https://cronfa.swan.ac.uk/Record/cronfa53997/Download/53997__17126__f5832f1a79734acb950ae493504c3a2c.pdf)
- Olfactory variant: "Inducing lucid dreams by olfactory-cued reactivation of reality testing during early-morning sleep: A proof of concept" (Consciousness and Cognition, 2020) — [ScienceDirect](https://sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1053810020300222) (results not retrieved)
- Paller lab: "Lucid dreams from reactivating mindfulness during REM sleep" (International Journal of Dream Research, 2024) — [PDF, Paller lab](https://faculty.wcas.northwestern.edu/paller/IJDR2024.pdf) (details not retrieved)

**Two-way communication with dreamers (Konkoly et al. 2021)**
- Konkoly et al., "Real-time dialogue between experimenters and dreamers during REM sleep", *Current Biology* 31(7):1417–1427.e6, 2021. A four-lab collaboration (Northwestern, Sorbonne/Paris, Osnabrück, Radboud) used PSG-verified REM in 36 individuals across 57 sessions. Participants included novices, frequent lucid dreamers and one patient with narcolepsy. They were given math problems and yes/no questions and answered with left-right eye movements or facial muscle signals. This showed that dreamers can understand questions, use working memory and produce answers ("interactive dreaming") — [Current Biology](https://www.cell.com/current-biology/fulltext/S0960-9822(21)00059-2); [EurekAlert press release](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/616714); [HAL](https://hal.science/hal-03154112v1)

**At-home TLR with a smartphone (Konkoly et al. 2024)**
- Konkoly, Whitmore, Mallett, Mazurek & Paller, "Provoking lucid dreams at home with sensory cues paired with pre-sleep cognitive training", *Consciousness and Cognition* 125 (Oct 2024), 103759, PMID 39278157 — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39278157/); [full-text PDF](https://faculty.wcas.northwestern.edu/paller/C&C24.pdf)
  - The method moved TLR "from a laboratory procedure to a smartphone-based procedure without polysomnography". The lab version had needed an experimenter watching PSG, to time cues to stable REM and titrate cue intensity so the sleeper did not wake — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39278157/); [Psychology Today](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/illusions-delusions-and-reality/202410/can-a-smartphone-app-trigger-lucid-dreaming)
  - Experiment 1 (single arm, 19 people, one week): LD rose from 0.74 per week to 2.11 per week, about three times as many — [Science News](https://www.sciencenews.org/article/phone-app-help-lucid-dreams); [PsyPost](https://www.psypost.org/lucid-dreaming-app-triples-users-awareness-in-dreams-study-finds/)
  - Experiment 2 (controlled): the same training, but on alternate nights the cues were replaced by dummy sounds or silence ("untrained" cues). TLR increased lucidity compared with the blinded control nights. Cues worked when they were the same sounds used in pre-sleep training. Sample size is reported inconsistently: Science News says 112, IFLScience 120; another summary says 50 completed the full 7-night protocol — [Science News](https://www.sciencenews.org/article/phone-app-help-lucid-dreams); [IFLScience](https://www.iflscience.com/you-may-be-able-to-learn-to-lucid-dream-armed-with-just-a-smartphone-76539); [Neuroscience News](https://neurosciencenews.com/lucid-dreaming-sensory-cues-27941/)
  - The app combines WBTB and TLR. Pre-sleep training paired the cues with a lucid mindset while lying in bed with eyes closed for about 20 minutes, falling asleep — [Medical Xpress](https://medicalxpress.com/news/2024-10-lucid-proven-methods-unveiled-scientists.html); [PsyPost](https://www.psypost.org/lucid-dreaming-app-triples-users-awareness-in-dreams-study-finds/)
  - The authors leave open whether cues work by evoking a lucid mindset or simply by raising arousal during sleep — [PsyPost](https://www.psypost.org/lucid-dreaming-app-triples-users-awareness-in-dreams-study-finds/)
- An earlier Northwestern study (recruiting 2022) used an Android app with a Fitbit Ionic/Versa/Sense to detect REM and play soft cue sounds; iOS users were excluded — [Gadgets & Wearables, 2022](https://gadgetsandwearables.com/2022/04/04/fitbit-lucid-dreaming/); [Phase Today](https://phasetoday.com/experiment-with-smartwatches-to-stimulate-ld/)
- The Paller lab has said its next step is wearable technology that prevents unwanted awakenings and determines when the user is likely in REM — [Northwestern Now, Oct 2024](https://news.northwestern.edu/stories/2024/10/leveraging-the-power-of-lucid-dreams)

**Paller lab follow-ups 2025–2026**
- Konkoly, Morris, Hurka, Martinez, Sanders & Paller, "Creative problem-solving after experimentally provoking dreams of unsolved puzzles during REM sleep", *Neuroscience of Consciousness* (niaf067), received Sep 2025, accepted Dec 2025, published 5 Feb 2026. Twenty frequent lucid dreamers (in lab) worked on puzzles that each had a unique soundtrack. During REM, the soundtracks of half the unsolved puzzles were replayed. Dreams about a problem helped people find solutions the next day, and cues affected dream content even without lucidity. Some participants signalled with eye movements or sniffing — [PDF](https://faculty.wcas.northwestern.edu/paller/NeuroCSC2026.pdf); [ScienceDaily, Feb 2026](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/02/260213223926.htm); [PsyPost](https://www.psypost.org/scientists-use-dream-engineering-to-boost-creative-problem-solving-during-rem-sleep/); [Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/researchers-are-basically-inceptioning-lucid-dreamers-with-brain-teasers-2000718065)
- TLR plus CBT for nightmares in narcolepsy: a pilot multiple-baseline study (n=6) in the *Journal of Sleep Research*, 2025. CBT for nightmares, alone or with TLR, significantly reduced nightmare frequency and severity. TLR was delivered in the lab — [PMC12012161](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12012161/); [PDF](https://faculty.wcas.northwestern.edu/paller/JSR2025.pdf)
- A 2026 Brain Sciences article (vol. 16) on LD induction through contemplative compassion practice has Morris and Paller as co-authors — [search summary; ScienceDirect review "New strategies for the cognitive science of dreaming"](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S136466132400264X) (exact URL of the Brain Sciences article not obtained)

**Multicentre study with wearable EEG (Esfahani, Dresler et al.)**
- Jafarzadeh Esfahani M., … Dresler M., "Highly effective verified lucid dream induction using combined cognitive-sensory training and wearable EEG: a multi-centre study", bioRxiv 10.1101/2024.06.21.600133 (June 2024). The later full-text version is titled "Inducing lucid dreaming with multisensory stimulation: a preregistered multi-center study" (2026 date). Sixty participants in the Netherlands, Italy and Canada; SSILD training plus TLR; 2-channel EEG (wearable headband) with the open-source Dreamento toolbox. In the Netherlands and Italy (n=40), 65% and 45% of participants had an SVLD. Sessions with REM cueing produced at least one SVLD in 45% (NL) and 35% (IT), vs 35% and 15% for sham sessions — [bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.06.21.600133v1); [full PDF](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.06.21.600133.full.pdf)
- Key quote from the paper: "our results do not show clear benefits of REM cueing over SSILD training alone" — [bioRxiv full PDF](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.06.21.600133.full.pdf)
- Dreamento: "an open-source dream engineering toolbox for sleep EEG wearables" (arXiv 2207.03977) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2207.03977)

**MIT Dormio / Targeted Dream Incubation (hypnagogia, not REM)**
- Haar Horowitz, Cunningham, Maes & Stickgold, "Dormio: A targeted dream incubation device", *Consciousness and Cognition* 83 (Aug 2020), 102938. An inexpensive, open-source wearable that repeatedly plays audio prompts during hypnagogia (N1) so the prompted topic enters dreams — [MIT Media Lab](https://www-prod.media.mit.edu/publications/dormio-a-targeted-dream-incubation-device/)
- Haar Horowitz, Esfahany, Stickgold & Maes, *Scientific Reports* 2023: participants given TDI prompts produced more creative stories than those who napped without a prompt or stayed awake. The glove tracks muscle tone, heart rate and skin conductance to detect N1 onset — [MIT News, May 2023](https://news.mit.edu/2023/sleep-sweet-spot-dreams-creativity-0515)
- Fitt Insider (May 2026) claims TDI "steers dreams with up to 92% success". This is a secondary source without a stated metric — [Fitt Insider](https://insider.fitt.co/dream-engineering-enters-wellness-neurotech-lucid-dreaming/)

### Inferences
- For an app, the most robust evidence-based core is TLR: pre-sleep training that pairs a specific sound with a reality check or lucid mindset, then quiet playback of the same sound in the second half of the night, ideally combined with WBTB, MILD or SSILD. Konkoly 2024 shows this can work without any sensor.
- The main risk of cueing is waking the sleeper. In the lab this was handled by titrating volume, and an app needs an equivalent mechanism (for example, the user marks "the cue woke me", or volume steps down automatically).
- The Esfahani/Dresler finding (no clear advantage of REM cueing over SSILD alone) suggests that a large share of the effect comes from the training and the WBTB protocol. The app should treat cues as an add-on, not the main mechanism.
- Dormio/TDI is a separate, easier-to-implement feature (sleep-onset prompts), suitable for dream incubation rather than lucidity itself.

### Gaps
- The exact cue-timing mechanism of the Konkoly 2024 app (fixed delays after WBTB? random intervals? volume adaptation?) was not obtained; the full text is in the PDF at faculty.wcas.northwestern.edu/paller/C&C24.pdf. The exact n for Experiment 2 is also unconfirmed (sources say 112, 120 or 50 completers).
- Results of the 2022 Northwestern Fitbit study (if published) were not found.
- Per-modality effectiveness (light vs sound vs vibration) from Tan & Fan 2023 and Stumbrys 2012 was not retrieved in detail.
- Results of the olfactory TLR study (2020) and the IJDR 2024 mindfulness TLR study were not retrieved.
- The TIME article "The New Science of Dreaming" (time.com, 18 Aug 2026) showed up in results, but its content could not be read.
- A 2026 bioRxiv preprint "A Lab-Based Lucid Dream Induction Paradigm Using …" (10.64898/2026.03.11.711049) appeared in results, but its authors and results are unknown.

## 2. Neurostimulation: tACS, tDCS, transcranial focused ultrasound (Prophetic), REMspace and others

### Takeaway
The "Voss effect" (40 Hz tACS induces lucidity, 2014) has not been replicated: the larger, sham-controlled Blanchette-Carrière 2020 study found no effect. tDCS (Stumbrys 2013) gave only a weak effect, and only in frequent lucid dreamers. Prophetic's transcranial focused ultrasound (tFUS) has no peer-reviewed efficacy data in 2026, yet it is sold on pre-order ("Dual" US$449, shipping end of 2026; "Phase" US$1,299, 2027). REMspace claims (2024) have not been independently verified. Neurostimulation is irrelevant for a phone app except as context for marketing claims.

### Cited Findings
- **Voss et al. 2014 (Nature Neuroscience, "Induction of self awareness in dreams through frontal low current stimulation of gamma activity")**: frontal 40 Hz tACS during REM increased self-reported lucidity, specifically at 40 Hz (and partly 25 Hz) and not at other frequencies. One blog states "77% of trials", which is unverified and probably a misinterpretation — [ResearchGate record](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/262227620_Induction_of_self_awareness_in_dreams_through_frontal_low_current_stimulation_of_gamma_activity); [Neurosity guide](https://neurosity.co/guides/lucid-dreaming-brain-states-eeg) (secondary)
- **Replication failure**: Blanchette-Carrière et al. 2020, "Attempted induction of signalled lucid dreaming by transcranial alternating current stimulation" (Consciousness and Cognition). n=40 (20 experienced lucid dreamers); compared with sham, 40 Hz tACS had no effect on LD induction (per the Tan & Fan 2023 review) — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/342080761_Attempted_induction_of_signalled_lucid_dreaming_by_transcranial_alternating_current_stimulation); [Tan & Fan 2023](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jsr.13786)
- **tDCS – Stumbrys, Erlacher & Schredl 2013** (Consciousness and Cognition): 19 participants, 3 lab nights; 1 mA tDCS over the DLPFC for 10 minutes vs sham in each REM period from the second onward. Self-reported lucidity was higher after stimulation, but "the effects were not strong and found only in frequent lucid dreamers". The authors call it preliminary support for DLPFC involvement and want control for indirect effects of stimulation — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1053810013001098); [PhilPapers](https://philpapers.org/rec/STUTTI)
- Consumer tACS headbands: LucidCatcher and Aladdin were the only headbands promising LD through frontal tACS — [Mota-Rolim et al. 2019](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6517539/)
- **Prophetic (Halo → Dual/Phase)**:
  - Oct 2023: Prophetic (co-founder Eric Wollberg) announced the "Halo" headband with EEG and transcranial ultrasound to induce and stabilise LD in REM, using an AI model "Morpheus-1" ("multi-modal generative ultrasonic transformer"). It raised US$1.1M (BoxGroup, a16z Scout Fund, Escape Velocity, O'Shaughnessy Ventures). It announced a partnership with the Donders Institute (Radboud), with data expected in spring 2024 and shipping in spring 2025 — [CNBC, 4 Oct 2023](https://www.cnbc.com/2023/10/04/ai-startup-prophetic-aims-to-build-headset-that-lets-you-control-dreams.html); [Fortune, 30 Nov 2023](https://fortune.com/2023/11/30/lucid-dream-startup-prophetic-headset-prepare-meetings-while-sleeping/); [ScienceAlert](https://www.sciencealert.com/halo-device-coming-in-2025-is-designed-to-induce-lucid-dreams)
  - Earlier estimated price: about US$1,500–2,000 — [Industry Tap](https://www.industrytap.com/unlocking-dreams-try-out-the-2000-ai-headband-with-dream-controlling-abilities/71414)
  - Scientific scepticism from the start — [Slate, Oct 2023](https://slate.com/technology/2023/10/prophetic-lucid-dreams-startup-artificial-intelligence.html); [Glitchwire](https://glitchwire.com/news/prophetics-halo-wants-to-put-lucid-dreaming-on-demand/)
  - 2026: pre-orders opened for two consumer devices: **Dual** (US$449, shipping Q4/late 2026) and **Phase** (US$1,299, shipping Q3/mid-late 2027). Low-intensity tFUS targeting the prefrontal cortex; Dual reportedly has two fixed ultrasound transducers and Fresnel-lens focusing. "No peer-reviewed efficacy data has been published ahead of ship date" — [Neurofounders – Prophetic](https://www.neurofounders.co/startups/prophetic); [Neurofounders article, May 2026](https://www.neurofounders.co/articles/prophetic-announces-a-dream-inducing-ultrasound-headband); [VFuture Media](https://vfuturemedia.com/gadgets/prophetic-ai-dream-headband-control-your-dreams-with-449-lucid-tech-2026/); [Substack analysis (L. Zaroff)](https://monismos.substack.com/p/prophetic-just-launched-a-new-brain); [Prophetic on X](https://x.com/PropheticAI?lang=en)
  - The company's X account says Dual is "now available to order" and its "first production run is nearly sold out" (company claims) — [Prophetic on X](https://x.com/PropheticAI?lang=en)
  - Fitt Insider (May 2026): "early tests show increased dream recall, clarity, and control, but peer-reviewed studies are still needed" — [Fitt Insider](https://insider.fitt.co/dream-engineering-enters-wellness-neurotech-lucid-dreaming/)
  - A 2026 buyer's guide (April 2026) said no consumer units had shipped at that point and that focused ultrasound "hasn't been validated at scale for lucid dream induction" — [World of Lucid Dreaming, Best devices 2026](https://www.world-of-lucid-dreaming.com/best-lucid-dreaming-devices-2026.html)
- **REMspace (Michael Raduga, California)**: claims that on 24 Sep 2024 two people in separate homes exchanged a random word in LD (via earbuds and "Remmyo", an EMG facial-muscle "dream language"), with a second run on 13 Oct 2024. Not peer-reviewed or independently verified; Raduga says he submitted it for review — [ABC7](https://abc7news.com/post/bay-area-startup-remspace-claims-developed-communication-between-people-dreams/15439111/); [The Debrief](https://thedebrief.org/lucid-dreaming-breakthrough-startup-claims-first-ever-two-way-dream-communication/); [Vice](https://www.vice.com/en/article/lucid-dreaming-tech-michael-raduga-remspace/)
  - Raduga made headlines in 2023 for drilling into his own skull to implant an electrode in an attempt to control dreams, and nearly died from blood loss — [Vice](https://www.vice.com/en/article/lucid-dreaming-tech-michael-raduga-remspace/)
  - No peer-reviewed REMspace publication or replication was found for 2025–2026 — (negative search result)

### Inferences
- Electrical stimulation (tACS/tDCS) has no robust evidence; Voss 2014 is cited mainly as a negative example of non-replication.
- Prophetic is a typical case of a marketing claim running ahead of evidence: a product on sale with no published efficacy or sleep-safety data. For an app it may be a future integration target at most, not an evidence base.
- Neurable (EEG headphones for focus) did not appear in any LD context; it is probably not relevant to LD (unverified).

### Gaps
- Exact numbers from Voss 2014 (n, % lucidity) and Blanchette-Carrière 2020 were not obtained from primary sources.
- Results of the Prophetic–Donders study have not been published (none found).
- No information was found on Neurable or other startups in an LD context.
- Safety data for tFUS during sleep were not found.

## 3. Pharmacology and supplements (with evidence grade)

### Takeaway
The only substance with a solid RCT for LD is **galantamine** (LaBerge et al. 2018: 121 participants, crossover, 14% → 27% at 4 mg → 42% at 8 mg, combined with MILD and WBTB). It is a prescription drug in the EU, including the Czech Republic, and has side effects (GI upset, insomnia, fatigue). Donepezil has only small studies; vitamin B6 improves dream recall but has not been tested for lucidity, and its replication is questioned. Herbs (Calea, Silene) have almost no human evidence, and Calea has toxicity signals and is a controlled substance in Poland. An app should not recommend prescription substances.

### Cited Findings
**Galantamine** — evidence grade: moderate (1 large RCT plus smaller studies; mostly from one research group)
- LaBerge, LaMarca & Baird 2018, PLOS ONE 13(8):e0201246: double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover; n=121 participants with high dream recall and interest in LD; 0, 4 and 8 mg in counterbalanced order (with MILD after WBTB). LD: 14% (active placebo procedure), 27% (4 mg), 42% (8 mg). Galantamine also increased dream recall, sensory vividness and complexity — [PubMed 30089135](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30089135/); [PMC6082533](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6082533/)
- Side effects in the same study: 14 participants (12%) reported mild side effects on an active dose: 12 (10%) at 8 mg, 11 (9%) at 4 mg, vs 4 (3%) on placebo (p=0.039 for 4 mg, p=0.057 for 8 mg). Types: mild GI upset, insomnia, next-day fatigue. Participants also reported "odd somatic sensations" (paralysis, tingling, vibrations), with no percentage given — [PMC6082533](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6082533/)
- A blog summary of LaBerge's earlier 2004 pilot work reports more sleep paralysis and a 40% increase in estimated time awake at night (secondhand, unverified) — [DreamStudies galantamine review](https://dreamstudies.org/galantamine-review-lucid-dreaming-pill/)
- Mechanism: galantamine is an acetylcholinesterase inhibitor and also a positive allosteric modulator of nicotinic receptors (unlike donepezil and rivastigmine) — [Brain Sciences 2024 narrative review, "Neuropsychopharmacological Induction of (Lucid) Dreams" (PMC11119155)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11119155/); [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3425/14/5/426)
- Sparrow et al. 2018 (Consciousness and Cognition, n=35): galantamine combined with meditation and dream reliving — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/326098818_Exploring_the_effects_of_galantamine_paired_with_meditation_and_dream_reliving_on_recalled_dreams_Toward_an_integrated_protocol_for_lucid_dream_induction_and_nightmare_resolution)
- Legal status: Reminyl (galantamine) is prescription-only in Europe (UK: POM; Spain: prescription required). In the US it was approved by the FDA in 2001 for mild to moderate Alzheimer's disease — [medicines.org.uk](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/medicine/10340); [oladoctor (ES)](https://oladoctor.com/en/medicines/spain/ingredient/galantamine); [Alzforum](https://alzforum.org/node/38535)
- Tan & Fan 2023 list galantamine as a "possible competitor" to MILD that needs replication — [Tan & Fan 2023](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jsr.13786)
- The 2024 narrative review concludes that galantamine is the main candidate, but more research is needed before any substance can reliably induce LD; the complexity of the cholinergic and dopaminergic systems limits interpretation — [PMC11119155](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11119155/)

**Donepezil** — evidence grade: low (small studies, n≈10)
- In a small crossover study, 8 of 10 (or 9 of 10 in another study) had an LD on donepezil vs 1 of 10 on placebo; it causes nausea — [PMC11119155](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11119155/)
- Rivastigmine and galantamine reportedly had the same effect on lucidity with fewer side effects — [PMC11119155](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11119155/)

**Huperzine A** — evidence grade: very low
- Appears to work similarly to donepezil, but at a much higher dose — [PMC11119155](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11119155/)
- An informal older "case study" (Yuschak) reports combinations such as galantamine + nicotine, galantamine + propranolol and huperzine + bupropion with high success, but it is anecdotal and uses small samples — [Substances that facilitate lucid dreaming (PDF)](https://sognilucidi.net/wp-content/uploads/2010/10/substances_that_facilitate_lucid_dreaming_-_a_case_study.pdf)

**Vitamin B6** — evidence grade: low for recall, none for lucidity
- Aspy, Madden & Delfabbro 2018, Perceptual and Motor Skills: double-blind RCT, about 100 Australians, 240 mg B6 before bed for 5 nights. B6 significantly increased the amount of dream content recalled; vividness, bizarreness, colour and other sleep measures did not change. Lucidity was not tested directly — [ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2018/04/180427100258.htm); [Medical News Today](https://www.medicalnewstoday.com/articles/321676)
- One commentary says the recall effect did not replicate (unclear source, unverified) — [Nightcone](https://nightcone.com/journal/vitamin-b6/)

**Calea zacatechichi** — evidence grade: very low, with safety concerns
- Mayagoitia et al. 1986 ("Psychopharmacologic analysis of an alleged oneirogenic plant"): subjective dream reports were higher than with placebo and diazepam, with more hypnagogic imagery in light sleep — [PubMed 3821139](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3821139/)
- Mouse study: neuropharmacological characterisation of the aqueous extract — [PMC4863909](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4863909/)
- 2024/2025 (J Ethnopharmacology): "Mexican calea… interferes with cholinergic and dopaminergic pathways and causes neuroglial toxicity" — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0378874124012145)
- Legal: controlled in Poland since 2009 (Dz.U. 2009 nr 63 poz. 520); not controlled federally in the US, but illegal in Louisiana (2005) for human consumption — [Erowid](https://www.erowid.org/plants/calea_zacatechichi/calea_zacatechichi_law.shtml) (Erowid itself warns to verify)
- Healthline: more human research is needed — [Healthline](https://www.healthline.com/health/calea-zacatechichi)

**Silene capensis/undulata (African dream root)** — evidence grade: none (ethnobotanical tradition only)
- Traditionally used by the Xhosa to induce vivid or "prophetic" dreams; no controlled human studies were found — [Wikipedia: Silene undulata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silene_undulata)

**Choline, alpha-GPC, melatonin, mugwort, nicotine patches** — no usable evidence was found in this session's searches (see Gaps).

**Clinical context**: a 2025/2026 narrative review says evidence for LD as therapy for PTSD and nightmares is preliminary and calls for larger RCTs — [PMC12889498](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12889498/)

### Inferences
- Evidence ranking: galantamine (moderate) > donepezil (low) > B6 for recall (low) > huperzine A, Calea (very low) > Silene, mugwort, choline, melatonin (none or anecdotal).
- In the Czech Republic, galantamine is available only on prescription (EU status). An app should present it only as information with a "consult a doctor" note, not as a recommendation. B6 at 240 mg is well above the usual daily dose (my inference; check against EFSA limits).
- Cholinergic substances also bring higher rates of sleep paralysis and fragmented sleep (anecdotal and pilot data), which matters for app safety warnings.

### Gaps
- Primary data on choline, alpha-GPC, melatonin, mugwort and nicotine patches for LD were not found (likely only anecdotes; not confirmed).
- The Czech legal status of Calea and galantamine as a supplement was not verified (only the EU prescription status of Reminyl).
- The exact citation and authors of the Kern et al. donepezil study were not obtained (only the summary in the narrative review).
- The B6 replication failure claim is unconfirmed.

## 4. Device catalogue (past and current)

### Takeaway
Almost all consumer LD devices from 2012–2020 (NovaDreamer, Aurora, Neuroon, LucidCatcher, Dreem for consumers) are discontinued or orphaned, and none has independent proof of effectiveness. In 2026 the realistic options are Remee (a cheap LED mask with a timer and no REM detection), ZMax/Hypnodyne (research-grade EEG headband, about US$900, used in published studies) and new pre-order products (Prophetic Dual/Phase, LucidMe PRO). ## 5. Accuracy of REM detection by phones and wearables, and feasibility of real-time cues (HealthKit, Health Connect, watchOS, Wear OS, Sleep as Android)

### Takeaway
Wrist and ring wearables detect sleep vs wake well (sensitivity ≥95%) but distinguish stages, including REM, only moderately (four-stage kappa about 0.2–0.65; Oura best at about 0.65 in a company-funded study). More importantly for an app, **neither HealthKit nor Health Connect provides sleep stages in real time**: Apple Watch and Samsung write stages only after waking (Apple only for sessions over 3 hours). Real-time cueing therefore requires your own algorithm on the watch (raw HR and accelerometer data inside a watchOS extended runtime session or a Wear OS app), or a cheap fallback: timed cues without detection (Konkoly 2024 shows this works).

### Cited Findings
**Validation against PSG**
- Brigham and Women's Hospital (Sensors, 2024; funded by Oura): Oura Gen3, Apple Watch and Fitbit Sense against PSG. Sleep/wake sensitivity ≥95% for all; stage sensitivity 50–86% (Oura 76.0–79.5%, Fitbit 61.7–78.0%, Apple 50.5–86.1%). Four-stage kappa: Oura 0.65, Apple Watch 0.60, Fitbit Sense 0.55. Oura did not differ significantly from PSG in estimating wake, light, deep or REM sleep. Caveat: funded by Oura — [PMC11511193](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11511193/); [Oura blog](https://ouraring.com/blog/2024-sensors-oura-ring-validation-study/); [Sleep Review](https://sleepreviewmag.com/sleep-diagnostics/consumer-sleep-tracking/wearable-sleep-trackers/oura-ring-apple-watch-fitbit-face-off-sleep-accuracy-study/)
- Six wrist devices (Fitbit Sense, Fitbit Charge 5, Withings, Garmin, Whoop, Apple Watch Series 8) vs PSG, *Sleep Advances* 2025 (zpaf021): all wearables identified deep sleep and REM epochs correctly more often than wake and light sleep; overall agreement was fair to moderate (kappa 0.21–0.53) — [PMC12038347](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12038347/); [OUP](https://academic.oup.com/sleepadvances/article/6/2/zpaf021/8090472)
- Lee et al. 2023, JMIR mHealth uHealth 11:e50983: 11 consumer trackers (wearable, nearable, airable, including Galaxy Watch 5, Pixel Watch, Oura 3 and Apple Watch 8), 3,890 hours of recordings and 543 hours of PSG. Four-stage macro F1 ranged from 0.26 to 0.69. Best overall was the SleepRoutine app (airable, F1 0.69; REM F1 0.76), followed by Amazon Halo Rise (0.62); best for deep sleep were Pixel Watch (0.59) and Fitbit Sense 2 (0.56). The study has disclosed ties to SleepRoutine's maker — [JMIR](https://mhealth.jmir.org/2023/1/e50983); [Zomni summary](https://zomni.app/blog/how-accurate-are-sleep-trackers)
- Samsung: a Galaxy Watch 3 validation vs PSG (n=32 healthy adults) exists in the Journal of Sleep Medicine (e-JSM), but its REM numbers were not obtained. No full 2024–2025 validation of Samsung REM staging was found — [e-JSM](https://www.e-jsm.org/journal/view.php?number=358)
- Apple: the white paper "Estimating Sleep Stages from Apple Watch" (updated Oct 2025) says stages are written only for sessions longer than 3 hours (1–3 hours give binary sleep/wake only) — [Apple PDF](https://www.apple.com/health/pdf/Estimating_Sleep_Stages_from_Apple_Watch_Oct_2025.pdf)
- Rankings vary between studies (Apple Watch was near the bottom for deep sleep in one and top in another); studies are small (about 20–75 mostly healthy adults) — [Ubie Health summary](https://ubiehealth.com/doctors-note/sleep-tracker-vs-polysomnography-accuracy-stage-4762q2)

**APIs and real-time limits**
- **HealthKit**: an Apple Developer Relations engineer confirmed on the Developer Forums that HealthKit only exposes HKCategoryValueSleepAnalysis and that sleep-stage data are not available in real time. The Apple Watch classifier runs in a private system process that apps cannot access while it runs — [Apple Developer Forums thread 804512](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/804512)
- HealthKit stage values since watchOS 9 / iOS 16 (WWDC22): asleepCore (N1+N2), asleepDeep (N3), asleepREM, asleepUnspecified (replaces the deprecated "asleep") — [WWDC22 "What's new in HealthKit"](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2022/10005/)
- In a smart alarm session, a developer could read HR but sleep analysis returned zeros. DTS: if rest lasts less than an hour, HealthKit generates no sleep samples — [Apple Developer Forums 772957](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/772957)
- **watchOS WKExtendedRuntimeSession – "smart alarm"** (WWDC 2019): the only session type that can be scheduled up to 36 hours ahead; runs for up to 30 minutes once started; must play a haptic at least once, or watchOS will offer the user to block the app's scheduling; meant for monitoring HR and motion changes in the window before waking. Each app supports only one session type (self care, mindfulness, physical therapy, smart alarm); a new session can only be started from the foreground; only one scheduled session at a time — [ASCIIwwdc – WWDC19 session 251](https://asciiwwdc.com/2019/sessions/251); [Apple Developer Forums 794730](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/794730); [Forum 682429](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/682429)
- AlarmKit (WWDC25, iOS 26) can forward alarm presentation to the paired watch — [Apple Developer Forums 794730](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/794730)
- A secondary source says iPhone-only sleep tracking was removed in iOS 18 (unverified) — [Sahha blog](https://sahha.ai/blog/sleep-stages-without-a-wearable/)
- **Health Connect (Android)**: SleepSessionRecord with stages UNKNOWN, AWAKE, SLEEPING, OUT_OF_BED, AWAKE_IN_BED, LIGHT, DEEP, REM; read by time range (ReadRecordsRequest); data are written per completed session — [Android Developers – Track sleep sessions](https://developer.android.com/health-and-fitness/health-connect/features/sleep-sessions); [SleepSessionRecord reference](https://developer.android.com/reference/kotlin/androidx/health/connect/client/records/SleepSessionRecord)
- Samsung: sleep data are created on the watch when the user wakes and are then transferred to the phone, so they are not real time — [Samsung Developer blog](https://developer.samsung.com/health/blog/en/managing-sleep-data-with-samsung-health-and-health-connect)
- **Sleep as Android** (Urbandroid): plays audio cues and gentle vibrations during estimated REM; REM is estimated from movement and sleep pattern ("it is not possible to directly identify REM phase from activity data alone"); false positives happen, so the developers recommend not starting cues in the first 2 hours. With a Huawei Watch 2, REM was not detected; with a Garmin Vivosmart 3 it was (forum) — [Sleep as Android Docs – Lucid dreaming](https://sleep.urbandroid.org/docs/sleep/lucid_dreaming.html); [Urbandroid forum](https://forum.urbandroid.org/t/lucid-dreaming-and-rem-detection-with-huawei-watch-2/2252); [Sleep lab comparison](https://sleep.urbandroid.org/sleep-lab-comparison/)

### Inferences
- **Architecture for an app (inference):**
  1. *Baseline without hardware (proven by Konkoly 2024)*: pre-sleep TLR training, then cues timed by the clock (WBTB alarm, then cues after a set delay in the REM-rich last third of the night), with low volume and a "woke me" button for adaptive volume. On iOS this requires an active audio session (background audio) through the night; on Android a foreground service.
  2. *Apple Watch*: HealthKit stages cannot be used in real time. You have to run your own classifier on raw HR (HRV) and accelerometer data inside a WKExtendedRuntimeSession. The smart alarm type is limited to 30 minutes, though it can be scheduled; self care/mindfulness sessions can be started only in the foreground. The haptic (taptic engine) is a natural cue. This approach is used by Lucidly and DreamKnock. Verify the current watchOS 26 limits.
  3. *Wear OS / Galaxy Watch*: an own app on the watch with the Health Services API or Samsung Health Sensor SDK (raw HR/IBI and accelerometer) plus vibration. Health Connect is only good for analysis after the night (personalising cue timing from past nights).
  4. *Oura, Whoop, Garmin, Fitbit*: their cloud APIs give stages only after syncing (inference based on the HealthKit/Health Connect pattern; real-time is not possible). They are useful for learning a user's typical REM timing and tuning the schedule.
- Wrist and ring REM accuracy (kappa up to about 0.6, REM F1 at best about 0.76 for the best non-wearable) is probably enough for probabilistic cue timing (more cues in likely REM), not for precise "only in REM". Given the Esfahani/Dresler result, precise REM timing may not even be critical.
- Watch haptics during sleep are a mild cue; the risk of waking can be offset by gradually increasing intensity (inference; not tested in any study found).

### Gaps
- No primary studies were obtained on the accuracy of a phone alone (accelerometer or microphone under the mattress or on the bed) for REM; search budget ran out. Lee 2023 shows that an "airable" app (SleepRoutine, microphone) achieved the best REM F1 (0.76), which suggests microphone-based staging is feasible.
- Exact REM sensitivity/specificity per device from the Sleep Advances 2025 and JMIR 2023 studies were not obtained (in the tables of the full texts).
- Current watchOS 26 limits for WKExtendedRuntimeSession were not verified in Apple documentation (only the WWDC19 talk and forums).
- Whether Garmin Connect IQ, Oura or Whoop offer any real-time HR stream for third-party apps during the night was not verified.
- Whether Google Pixel Watch or Fitbit offer a real-time sleep-stage API (they appear not to) was not confirmed.

## 6. Trends 2023–2026 and emerging directions

### Takeaway
Since 2023 the field has moved from lab PSG to the home: a smartphone app (Konkoly 2024), wearable EEG with open-source software (Dreamento/ZMax, the Esfahani/Dresler multicentre study), and use of REM cues beyond lucidity itself (creative problem solving, nightmare therapy). At the same time, venture-funded "dream engineering" with neuromodulation is growing (Prophetic tFUS on pre-order, REMspace) without peer-reviewed evidence, alongside consumer closed-loop EEG headbands for sleep (Elemind). The research trend is towards preregistered multicentre studies that reveal smaller effects than early pilots.

### Cited Findings
- Paller lab: 2024 smartphone TLR at home; 2025 TLR for narcolepsy nightmares; 2026 REM cueing for creative problem solving (Neuroscience of Consciousness); planned wearable for REM detection that avoids awakenings — [C&C 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39278157/); [JSR 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12012161/); [NeuroCSC 2026](https://faculty.wcas.northwestern.edu/paller/NeuroCSC2026.pdf); [Northwestern Now](https://news.northwestern.edu/stories/2024/10/leveraging-the-power-of-lucid-dreams)
- Preregistered multicentre study with wearable EEG: high SVLD rates, but REM cueing showed no clear benefit over SSILD — [bioRxiv Esfahani/Dresler](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.06.21.600133.full.pdf)
- Open-source "dream engineering" software for EEG wearables (Dreamento) — [arXiv 2207.03977](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2207.03977)
- "Dream engineering enters wellness neurotech" (May 2026): Prophetic DTC headbands at US$449–1,299 combining EEG and tFUS; TDI from MIT — [Fitt Insider, 1 May 2026](https://insider.fitt.co/dream-engineering-enters-wellness-neurotech-lucid-dreaming/)
- Consumer closed-loop EEG with acoustic stimulation for sleep (Elemind, US$349–399) — [Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/wellness/sleep-tech/elemind-sleep-headband)
- Dreem moved from consumer to clinical/pharma use (Beacon Biosignals, FDA 510(k) for Dreem 3S) — [Circatest](https://circatest.com/headbands/dreem-3s-review)
- AI sleep staging from sound (airable apps) reached the best REM F1 among consumer trackers in 2023 — [Lee et al. 2023, JMIR](https://mhealth.jmir.org/2023/1/e50983)
- An open multimodal EEG dataset for decoding dreams with AI ("Dream2Image", arXiv 2510.06252, 2025) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.06252)
- Micro-phenomenology of SVLD ("Becoming Lucid: A Micro-Phenomenological Investigation of Signal-Verified Lucid Dreams", 2025) — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/397680378_Becoming_Lucid_A_Micro-Phenomenological_Investigation_of_Signal-Verified_Lucid_Dreams)
- HCI design research on LD induction (LuciEntry, ACM 2024 and 2025) — [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3715336.3735790)
- Competing LD apps market themselves as "cueing within REM" based on trackers (e.g. Ludin), a sign of commercial interest in the wearable-plus-app segment — [Ludin vs sleep trackers](http://ludin.app/learn/ludin-vs-sleep-trackers)

### Inferences
- The market window for an app: dedicated LD hardware has mostly failed, and new hardware (Prophetic) is expensive and unproven. Meanwhile there is strong academic evidence for a software protocol (TLR plus WBTB plus MILD/SSILD) that works on a phone. An app that implements the Konkoly 2024 protocol, optionally refined with a wearable, is consistent with the current state of the science.
