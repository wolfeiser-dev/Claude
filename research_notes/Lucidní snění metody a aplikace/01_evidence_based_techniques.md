## Q1. Catalogue of techniques: origin, exact protocol, evidence

### Takeaway
MILD is the only cognitive technique that is consistently effective across several independent studies. It works best combined with a short sleep interruption (WBTB) roughly 5–6 h after sleep onset, followed by quickly falling back asleep. SSILD has one supportive large field study. Reality testing alone, autosuggestion alone and WBTB alone have weak or null controlled evidence. WILD, FILD, DEILD and CAT are community techniques with no controlled trials.

### Cited Findings

#### 1. MILD (Mnemonic Induction of Lucid Dreams)
- **Origin:** Stephen LaBerge developed MILD in his Stanford PhD work. He described it as a single-subject case study, with himself as the subject, in *Perceptual and Motor Skills*: LaBerge, S. (1980). Lucid dreaming as a learnable skill: A case study. 51(3), 1039–1042. https://doi.org/10.2466/pms.1980.51.3f.1039 **[PK]**
- **Mechanism (as proposed by its authors):** MILD relies on **prospective memory**, i.e. remembering to do something in the future: "remember to notice you are dreaming". This framing appears in Aspy et al. 2017 and ILDIS 2020 — [Aspy et al. 2017, Dreaming](https://doi.org/10.1037/drm0000059) **[PK framing; paper existence V]**
- **Protocol, LaBerge's canonical form (LaBerge & Rheingold 1990, *Exploring the World of Lucid Dreaming*, Ballantine)** **[PK]**
  1. *Set up dream recall:* before sleep, resolve to wake up and remember dreams during the night.
  2. *Recall the dream:* on waking from a dream at any time, especially early morning, recall it in as much detail as possible.
  3. *Focus intent:* while returning to sleep, concentrate single-mindedly on the intention. Canonical wording: **"Next time I'm dreaming, I want to remember to recognise that I'm dreaming."** Use the words to form the intention; do not mumble them mechanically.
  4. *See yourself becoming lucid:* imagine being back in the dream you just recalled. Notice a "dreamsign", an anomaly in the dream such as an impossible event, and say to yourself "I'm dreaming!". Then imagine carrying out a planned in-dream action, for example flying.
  5. *Repeat* steps 3–4 until the intention feels firmly set, or until you fall asleep.
- **Protocol as used in the Australian studies (Aspy 2017; ILDIS 2020)** **[PK; summary V]**: an alarm wakes the participant about **5 hours after going to bed** (WBTB). The participant recalls the dream, then repeats the phrase "The next time I'm dreaming, I will remember that I'm dreaming" while imagining being back in the dream and becoming lucid. They repeat this until confident the intention is set, then go to sleep. Press coverage describes the 2017 MILD as waking "after about five hours of sleep" and rehearsing the intention before going back to sleep — [ScienceDaily / Univ. Adelaide 2017](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2017/10/171019100812.htm) **[V]**
- **Evidence:**
  - Tan & Fan's 2023 systematic review concluded that **MILD was the most effective technique**, inducing LDs successfully in **6 studies** among the 19 reviewed — [Tan & Fan 2023, J Sleep Res, doi:10.1111/jsr.13786](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jsr.13786) **[V]**
  - In the Australian study, the RT+WBTB+MILD group (n = 47) went from **9.4% of nights lucid at baseline to 17.4%** in a single practice week. Participants who fell asleep **within 5 minutes** of finishing MILD succeeded on **about 46% of attempts** (45.8%) — [Univ. Adelaide press release](https://adelaide.edu.au/news/news95682.html); [summary of results](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9149976/) **[V]**
  - ILDIS 2020 (N = 355): **MILD ≈ SSILD in effectiveness** — [Aspy 2020, Front Psychol, doi:10.3389/fpsyg.2020.01746](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7379166/) **[V]**
  - In the sleep lab, WBTB + MILD (with dream work) gave LDs to **about half** of participants not selected for LD ability, and **about one third** produced objectively verified LDs (eye signals) — [Erlacher & Stumbrys 2020, Front Psychol, doi:10.3389/fpsyg.2020.01383](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2020.01383/full) **[V]**
- **Evidence grade:** **Moderate.** There are several small controlled lab studies plus large field studies with within-subject baselines. MILD appears in systematic reviews. There is no large blinded RCT of MILD vs. an active control.

#### 2. WBTB (Wake Back To Bed / sleep interruption)
- **Origin:** LaBerge's lab work in the 1980s–90s on early-morning awakenings and naps. LaBerge, Phillips & Levitan (1994), "An hour of wakefulness before morning naps makes lucidity more likely", *NightLight* 6(3), Lucidity Institute (non-peer-reviewed newsletter) **[PK]**
- **Rationale:** REM periods grow longer and more intense later in the night. A brief awakening may raise cortical arousal and prefrontal activation, and it gives the person a chance to rehearse an intention just before REM-rich sleep. Gott et al. found that self-reported awakenings, polyphasic sleep and **physiologically verified wake→REM transitions** were associated with LD. Self-rated sleep quality and physiologically measured awakenings were not — [Gott et al. 2020, Consc Cogn 84:102988](https://boris.unibe.ch/189104/1/1-s2.0-S1053810019305227-main.pdf) **[V]**
- **Timing evidence:**
  - In the lab, **6 h of sleep, then wake, then about 1 h awake doing dream work plus MILD** was the most effective condition. The authors summarised it as "the most effective approach is to use 1 h WBTB time, during which dreamwork is carried out and MILD is practiced." In the control conditions without LD instructions, the reading condition gave 9% (1 participant) and the Wii-gaming condition 0% — [Erlacher & Stumbrys 2020](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2020.01383/full) **[V]**
  - **Earlier interruption works worse.** In three replication experiments, sleep before waking was shortened from 6 h to **4.5 h**, awakenings were time-based rather than REM-based, and RT replaced MILD. LD rates were **26% (4/15), 0% (0/20) and 20% (3/15)**, against 53% for MILD in the original study. Without REM awakenings in the morning, induction failed, and reality testing did worse than MILD — [Erlacher, Furrer, Ineichen, Braillard & Schmid 2022, Clocks & Sleep 4(2):230–239, doi:10.3390/clockssleep4020021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9149976/) **[V]**
  - **WBTB alone (no cognitive technique) at home is not effective.** Online trial (N = 153 recruited, 138 analysable): 5 h of sleep, then 30 min awake reading a text for a memory task, then back to bed. Only **4.3%** reported an LD in the study night, within the expected spontaneous rate of 2.5–4.3%. 63% already had LD experience, and **all 8 who had an LD were experienced** lucid dreamers. Single-group design, no control — [Klofat et al. 2024, Somnologie, doi:10.1007/s11818-024-00453-8](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11818-024-00453-8) **[V]**
  - One summary cited in search results reported **18% LDs after WBTB nights vs. 6% after non-WBTB nights** in a home setting (Dreaming 2020, doi:10.1037/drm0000152). The primary paper could not be retrieved, so authors and design are unverified — [summary via PMC9149976 search snippet](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9149976/) **[V-snippet only; primary unverified]**
  - In the galantamine RCT, the placebo arm (WBTB at about 4.5 h, ≥30 min awake, MILD) reached about **14%** LD (vs. 27% with 4 mg and 42% with 8 mg). This is a useful benchmark for WBTB+MILD in motivated, experienced dreamers (N = 121) — LaBerge, LaMarca & Baird 2018, PLoS ONE 13(8):e0201246, https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0201246 **[PK]**
- **Practical parameters implied by the evidence:** wake about **4.5–6 h** after sleep onset; 6 h did better in the Erlacher series. Awake periods of **30–60 min** were used in the lab, with 60 min best in Erlacher & Stumbrys 2020. Home studies used shorter or no fixed wake periods but required falling asleep quickly (Aspy). (Inference; see Q3.)
- **Evidence grade:** **Moderate as a component** (WBTB + MILD). **Weak/null as a stand-alone** technique (Klofat 2024).

#### 3. Reality testing (RT) / reality checks
- **Origin:** Paul Tholey's "reflection technique" (critical questioning "Am I dreaming?") — Tholey, P. (1983). Techniques for inducing and manipulating lucid dreams. *Perceptual and Motor Skills* 57(1), 79–90. https://doi.org/10.2466/pms.1983.57.1.79 **[PK]**. LaBerge popularised the "state test" variants (re-reading text, digital clocks, light switches) in *Exploring the World of Lucid Dreaming* (1990) **[PK]**
- **Typical research protocol (Aspy 2017 / ILDIS)** **[PK]**: **at least 10 times a day**: (a) ask "Am I dreaming?"; (b) look around for anything odd or dream-like; (c) **try to breathe through a pinched nose** (in dreams you usually can breathe); (d) **read a line of text, look away and re-read it** (in dreams the text tends to change). Schredl's 2025 trial had participants train about **3 min per day** — [IJoDR 2025](https://journals.ub.uni-heidelberg.de/index.php/IJoDR/article/view/110523) **[V]**
- **Which checks work best:** I found no study that directly compares individual checks (nose-pinch vs. finger-through-palm vs. text/clock vs. light switch). Community and LaBerge recommendations favour the nose-pinch and text re-reading because they are quick and give clear-cut results in dreams. This rests on expert opinion and anecdote, not data. See Gaps.
- **Evidence:**
  - **RT alone did not increase LD** in the Australian study. Only the RT+WBTB+MILD group improved significantly, and the other groups did not change — [PMC9149976 summary of Aspy 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9149976/); [ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2017/10/171019100812.htm) **[V]**
  - **2025 RCT-type diary study** (N = 81, 59 women and 22 men, random assignment to RT or autosuggestion, 4 weeks, about 3 min/day, 28-day morning checklist). **Neither technique significantly increased LD frequency.** The apparent advantage of RT over autosuggestion (medium effect) was almost entirely explained by baseline LD frequency. Prior LD frequency was the main predictor — [Schredl 2025, IJoDR 18(2):201–206, doi:10.11588/ijodr.2025.2.110523](https://journals.ub.uni-heidelberg.de/index.php/IJoDR/article/view/110523) **[V]**
  - In the lab, RT after WBTB did worse than MILD — [Erlacher et al. 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9149976/) **[V]**
- **Evidence grade:** **Weak as a stand-alone technique.** Two controlled studies (Aspy 2017; Schredl 2025) found no significant effect on its own. It is plausibly useful as a component and is included in all ILDIS arms.

#### 4. SSILD (Senses-Initiated Lucid Dream)
- **Origin:** an online developer known as "CosmicIron", who posted the technique on the web (c. late 2000s / early 2010s). Aspy's ILDIS was the first formal test **[PK]**
- **Protocol (creator's / ILDIS version)** **[PK]**: done after a WBTB awakening, in a relaxed, passive attitude. Each cycle moves attention through three senses: **sight** (eyes closed, observe darkness and phosphenes, no straining), **hearing** (listen to ambient or inner sounds), **touch/body** (heaviness, warmth, tingling). Start with about **4–6 fast cycles of a few seconds per sense**, then about **3–4 slow cycles of about 30 s per sense**. Then fall asleep normally, with the expectation of becoming lucid, including possible false awakenings to test. The exact second-counts vary between versions; check against the ILDIS methods section.
- **Evidence:**
  - ILDIS (N = 355): **SSILD was as effective as MILD**. A MILD+SSILD hybrid **gave no added benefit** — [Aspy 2020](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7379166/) **[V]**
  - A blog reports per-attempt rates of **16.9% for SSILD vs. 16.5% for MILD**. **Not confirmed** in the primary paper — [oneironauts.io (secondary, unverified)](https://oneironauts.io/blog/lucid-dreaming-statistics) **[V that the blog says this; figure unverified]**
  - Tan & Fan 2023 rated SSILD **"insufficient/ambiguous"** because only one study (ILDIS) supports it — [Tan & Fan 2023](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jsr.13786) **[V]**
  - A 2024 multi-centre preprint used **SSILD as the pre-sleep cognitive component** combined with sensory cues during REM ("targeted lucidity reactivation") and wearable EEG, in morning naps. v1 (N = 40, NL and IT) had **65% (NL) and 45% (IT)** signal-verified LD. The revised version (N = 60, adding Canada) had **51.7%** (31/60) across two naps. Sham-cue sessions also produced LDs (v1: 35% NL, 15% IT vs. 45% and 35% with REM cueing). **Not peer reviewed** — [bioRxiv 10.1101/2024.06.21.600133](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.06.21.600133.full.pdf) **[V]**
- **Evidence grade:** **Limited/moderate.** One large field study, used as a component in lab protocols. No independent replication of SSILD alone.

#### 5. WILD (Wake-Initiated Lucid Dream) and hypnagogic-imagery techniques
- **Origin:** LaBerge distinguished DILD from WILD in the 1980s (LaBerge 1985, *Lucid Dreaming*, Tarcher; LaBerge & Rheingold 1990). Tholey's "image technique" and "body technique" (Tholey 1983) are early WILD variants **[PK]**
- **Protocol variants (LaBerge & Rheingold 1990)** **[PK]**: lie still and relaxed, usually after WBTB or in a morning or afternoon nap. Keep a thread of awareness while the body falls asleep, for example by counting "1, I'm dreaming; 2, I'm dreaming…". Alternatively, passively watch hypnagogic imagery without trying to engage it until it becomes an immersive scene ("hypnagogic imagery technique"), or imagine a body movement such as floating or rolling out of the body. Sleep paralysis and vibrations may occur and are harmless.
- **Mechanism support:** people with narcolepsy, who often enter REM straight from wake (sleep-onset REM), report LDs more often — Dodet, Chavez, Leu-Semenescu, Golmard & Arnulf 2015, *Sleep* 38(3):487–497, https://doi.org/10.5665/sleep.4516 **[PK]**. Wake→REM transitions are linked to LD — [Gott et al. 2020](https://boris.unibe.ch/189104/1/1-s2.0-S1053810019305227-main.pdf) **[V]**
- **Evidence grade:** **Anecdotal / case reports.** I found no controlled trial of WILD as a home technique for novices. Stumbrys 2012 included only a few small studies of wake-initiated approaches **[PK]**.

#### 6. FILD (Finger-Induced Lucid Dream)
- **Origin:** internet forums (DreamViews, c. 2010s); no academic author **[PK]**.
- **Protocol (community)** **[PK]**: after WBTB, while drowsy, alternately press the index and middle fingers very slightly, as if playing piano keys with almost no movement, for about 20–30 s. Then do a reality check (nose-pinch). If the check shows a dream, you are lucid.
- **Evidence grade:** **Anecdotal only.** No peer-reviewed study found. Tan & Fan's list of 14 techniques does not appear to include it (could not verify full text).

#### 7. DEILD (Dream-Exit-Induced LD) / "dream chaining"
- **Origin:** LaBerge described "dream-chaining" (*Exploring the World of Lucid Dreaming*, 1990): on waking from a dream, stay motionless and go straight back in. "DEILD" is the forum name **[PK]**.
- **Protocol (community)** **[PK]**: on any natural awakening, do not move or open your eyes. Keep your attention on the dream scene or the body sensation for a few seconds, then drift straight back in, ideally with an intention ("I'm re-entering a dream"). Often done with a reality check after re-entry.
- **Evidence grade:** **Anecdotal.**

#### 8. DILD (Dream-Initiated LD)
- **Definition:** becoming lucid from inside a dream, typically triggered by noticing an anomaly or doing a reality check in the dream. It is the outcome category for MILD, RT and dream-sign training, not a protocol in itself (LaBerge 1985) **[PK]**. All the field studies above (Aspy, Schredl) mainly measure DILD-type outcomes.

#### 9. Autosuggestion
- **Origin:** Tholey (1983); German research tradition (Schlag-Gies 1992 dissertation) **[PK]**.
- **Protocol:** in a relaxed state before sleep, repeat a suggestion such as "Tonight I will have a lucid dream / I will realise that I'm dreaming", without effortful willing. Tholey contrasted this with intention techniques **[PK]**.
- **Evidence:** 2025 four-week randomised diary study: **no significant increase** in LD frequency — [Schredl 2025](https://journals.ub.uni-heidelberg.de/index.php/IJoDR/article/view/110523) **[V]**
- **Evidence grade:** **Weak/null.** One controlled study was null, and older studies are small and low quality (Stumbrys 2012) **[PK]**.

#### 10. Intention setting / reflection–intention training (Tholey's reflection technique, Paulsson & Parker)
- **Tholey's reflection technique** **[PK]**: during the day, develop a "critical-reflective attitude". Ask "Am I dreaming or not?" **5–10 times a day**, especially in situations that resemble dreams or are unusual, and also just before falling asleep. Tholey's view was that the habit carries over into dreams.
- **Tholey's combined technique** **[PK]**: reflection technique, plus intention (imagining doing the RT in a dream situation), plus autosuggestion, plus focusing on an intended dream action. Tholey (1983) claimed this was the most effective of his methods. The claims are largely anecdotal or uncontrolled.
- **Paulsson & Parker 2006**: a two-week reflection–intention programme produced a small, mostly non-significant increase in LD recall — *Dreaming* 16(1):22–35, https://doi.org/10.1037/1053-0797.16.1.22 **[PK]**.
- **Purcell, Mullington, Moffitt, Hoffmann & Pigeau 1986**: attention–intention training increased dream self-reflectiveness, with some lucidity gains — *Sleep* 9(3):423–437, https://doi.org/10.1093/sleep/9.3.423 **[PK]**.
- **Evidence grade:** **Small controlled studies, low quality (Stumbrys 2012).**

#### 11. "Critical State Testing"
- This is Tholey's term (critical-reflective attitude, "kritische Reflexion") and LaBerge's term ("state testing") for the same family as RT and the reflection technique. It is not a separate validated protocol **[PK]**. The evidence is the same as for RT and reflection.

#### 12. Prospective memory (PM) training
- **Origin:** LaBerge's PM exercise in *Exploring the World of Lucid Dreaming* (1990) **[PK]**: choose a list of daily target events (e.g. "next time I see a dog / hear a phone / pass a door, I will do a state test"). Note whether you remembered, and practise over about a week before MILD. It is designed as preparation for MILD.
- **Evidence:** the Australian studies treated PM as the mechanism of MILD and measured PM-related variables (Aspy et al. 2017) **[PK]**. I found no published controlled trial of PM training alone as an LD intervention. See Gaps.
- **Evidence grade:** **Theoretical / indirect.**

#### 13. Dream journaling and dream-sign training
- **Origin:** LaBerge (1985, 1990) catalogued dream signs into four categories: inconsistency, form, context and action, plus awareness (inner experience). Users record their dreams and then target their most frequent personal dream signs in MILD and RT **[PK]**.
- **Evidence:** it is part of almost every induction protocol. Better dream recall predicts LD success (ILDIS) — [Aspy 2020](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7379166/) **[V]**. No trial isolates journaling as an LD intervention.
- **Evidence grade:** **Indirect** (a recall prerequisite; see Q5).

#### 14. CAT (Cycle Adjustment Technique)
- **Origin:** Daniel Love, *Are You Dreaming? Exploring Lucid Dreams: A Comprehensive Guide* (2013, Enchanted Loom) **[PK]**.
- **Protocol (Love)** **[PK]**: week 1, wake **90 min earlier** than usual every day (adaptation phase). Then alternate: on "on" days wake 90 min early, on "off" days wake at the normal time. The body is expected to be "primed" to wake at the early time, and on normal days the person does RTs during the late-morning sleep window or sleeps through it, which is expected to raise alertness in the last REM cycle.
- **Evidence grade:** **Anecdotal; no peer-reviewed study found.**

#### 15. Naps and sleep interruption
- Morning naps after an awakening are the standard lab context for high LD rates: Erlacher & Stumbrys 2020; Appel et al. 2020; Carr et al. 2020; the 2024 multi-centre preprint; Peters et al. 2026 preprint **[V for each, see Q2]**. Gott et al. 2020 found polyphasic sleep associated with LD — [Gott et al. 2020](https://boris.unibe.ch/189104/1/1-s2.0-S1053810019305227-main.pdf) **[V]**.

#### 16. Mindfulness / meditation (brief)
- Weak positive correlation between trait mindfulness and LD frequency — Stumbrys, Erlacher & Malinowski 2015, *Imagination, Cognition and Personality* 34(4):415–433, https://doi.org/10.2190/IC.34.4.e **[PK]**.
- Long-term meditators reported more LDs, but an **8-week MBSR course did not increase LD frequency** — Baird, Riedner, Boly, Davidson & Tononi 2019, *Psychology of Consciousness* 6(1):40–54, https://doi.org/10.1037/cns0000176 **[PK]**.
- Northwestern (Paller lab) paper: "Lucid dreams from reactivating mindfulness during REM sleep" (IJoDR 2024). Pre-sleep mindfulness training was paired with cues that were then replayed in REM — [PDF](https://faculty.wcas.northwestern.edu/paller/IJDR2024.pdf) **[V that it exists; numbers not retrieved]**.
- **Evidence grade:** **Correlational, plus one null training study.** As a cue-paired training it is promising but only preliminary.

### Inferences
- For an app: the **core protocol should be MILD after WBTB (or SSILD as an alternative)**, done roughly 5–6 h after sleep onset, with a short awake period and a strong push to **fall asleep fast** afterwards. RT and journaling should be supporting habits, not standalone promises.
- **Offer SSILD as an alternative to MILD.** The ILDIS data show no difference, and some users find SSILD's passive attention easier than MILD's effortful rehearsal. Combining the two adds nothing (hybrid ≈ either).

### Gaps
- **No head-to-head comparison of individual reality checks** (nose-pinch vs. finger-through-palm vs. clock/text vs. light switch) was found. Any app claim about "the best check" is expert opinion.
- Exact SSILD second-counts and exact ILDIS wording could not be read from the full text (fetch blocked).
- No controlled studies of FILD, DEILD, CAT, or WILD for novices at home.
- "Adventure-Dreams" or similar online induction programmes: nothing identifiable was found in the searches made. I can't confirm the name or any evaluation.

## Q2. Key studies with numbers (chronological)

### Takeaway
High-quality evidence is scarce. The 2012 review found that most studies were of poor quality and no technique was reliable. The 2023 review again ranked MILD best. Recent field trials (2024–2025) of single techniques (WBTB alone, RT alone, autosuggestion alone) are mostly null. Lab protocols combining WBTB, cognitive training and naps reach 40–50%+ in small samples.

### Cited Findings
- **Tholey 1983**, *Perceptual and Motor Skills* 57:79–90. Described reflection, intention, autosuggestion, the combined technique and the image/body techniques. Uncontrolled reports. https://doi.org/10.2466/pms.1983.57.1.79 **[PK]**
- **LaBerge 1980**, *Percept Mot Skills* 51:1039–1042. MILD single-case study. https://doi.org/10.2466/pms.1980.51.3f.1039 **[PK]**
- **Purcell et al. 1986**, *Sleep* 9(3):423–437. Attention/intention training. https://doi.org/10.1093/sleep/9.3.423 **[PK]**
- **Paulsson & Parker 2006**, *Dreaming* 16(1):22–35. Reflection–intention, 2 weeks. https://doi.org/10.1037/1053-0797.16.1.22 **[PK]**
- **Stumbrys, Erlacher, Schädlich & Schredl 2012**, *Consciousness and Cognition* 21(3):1456–1475, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.concog.2012.07.003. Systematic review of **35 studies: 26 cognitive, 11 external stimulation, 1 drug**. "None of the induction techniques were verified to induce lucid dreams reliably and consistently, although some of them look promising." **Most (60%) studies were of poor methodological quality** — [summary via search](https://api.philpapers.org/rec/STUIOL); [ResearchGate record](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/230581369_Induction_of_lucid_dreams_A_systematic_review_of_evidence) **[V]**
- **Saunders, Roe, Smith & Clegg 2016**, *Consciousness and Cognition* 43:197–215, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.concog.2016.06.002. Prevalence meta-analysis; see Q4 — [NECTAR repository](https://nectar.northampton.ac.uk/id/eprint/8705/) **[V]**
- **Aspy, Delfabbro, Proeve & Mohr 2017**, *Dreaming* 27(3):206–231, https://doi.org/10.1037/drm0000059 ("National Australian Lucid Dream Induction Study"). **N = 169**. Week 1 baseline log, week 2 practice. Three groups: RT; RT+WBTB; RT+WBTB+MILD. **RT+WBTB+MILD (n = 47): 9.4% → 17.4% of nights lucid.** Participants who fell asleep **within 5 min** of MILD succeeded on **~46% (45.8%) of attempts**. Successful MILD users were *less* sleep-deprived the next day. Other groups showed no significant change — [Univ. Adelaide](https://adelaide.edu.au/news/news95682.html); [ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2017/10/171019100812.htm); [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Reality-Testing-and-the-Mnemonic-Induction-of-Lucid-Aspy-Delfabbro/9cb99be433c3b1a9d5c8b5e0b3ab59c80a597dcb) **[V]**
- **LaBerge, LaMarca & Baird 2018**, *PLoS ONE* 13(8):e0201246. Galantamine double-blind crossover (drug study; covered by another researcher). The **placebo + WBTB + MILD arm gave ~14% LD** (N = 121). https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0201246 **[PK]**
- **Baird, Mota-Rolim & Dresler 2019**, *Neuroscience & Biobehavioral Reviews* 100:305–323, "The cognitive neuroscience of lucid dreaming". Reviews LD neural correlates (prefrontal/parietal activation, gamma debates) and induction. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neubiorev.2019.03.008 **[PK]**
- **Aspy 2020 (ILDIS)**, *Frontiers in Psychology* 11:1746, https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2020.01746. **N = 355** completed the pre-test, then a 1-week baseline log, then 1 week of practice. Arms: RT + WBTB with **MILD, SSILD, or a MILD+SSILD hybrid**. MILD ≈ SSILD; the hybrid had no advantage. Predictors of success: **better general dream recall** and **falling asleep within 10 min** of finishing the technique. Successful induction did not harm sleep quality — [PMC7379166](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7379166/); [PubMed 32765385](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32765385/) **[V]**. *Note:* the "5 minutes" figure belongs to Aspy 2017. ILDIS used a 10-minute threshold **[V]**.
- **Carr, Konkoly, Mallett, Edwards, Appel & Blagrove 2020**, *Psychology of Consciousness: Theory, Research, and Practice*, "Combining presleep cognitive training and REM-sleep stimulation in a laboratory morning nap for lucid dream induction", https://doi.org/10.1037/cns0000227 **[PK citation]**. Morning nap; pre-sleep training paired light and sound cues with lucid-mindset rehearsal; cues replayed in REM. **50% of cued participants produced a signal-verified LD** — [search snippet](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2020.01383/full); [Swansea Cronfa preprint](https://cronfa.swan.ac.uk/Record/cronfa53997/Download/53997__17126__f5832f1a79734acb950ae493504c3a2c.pdf) **[V-snippet]**. Cueing is a device element, but the pre-sleep cognitive training can be done without devices.
- **Erlacher & Stumbrys 2020**, *Frontiers in Psychology* 11:1383, https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2020.01383. Sleep lab; WBTB after about 6 h. Study 1: 11 sport students who had attended a sleep/dreams seminar. Study 2: 15 participants recruited to avoid that bias. **Best condition: 1 h WBTB with dream work + MILD, giving ~50% reported LD and ~1/3 eye-signal verified.** Reading control: 9%; Wii control: 0% — [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2020.01383/full); [PMC7332853](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7332853/) **[V]**
- **Appel, Füllhase, Kern, Kleinschmidt, Laukemper, Lüth, Steinmetz & Vogelsang 2020**, *Consciousness and Cognition* 83:102960, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.concog.2020.102960. **20 novices** with no LD experience, 1–2 lab nights. Woken from **REM after ~6 h**, did a set of cognitive tasks, then back to sleep. **10/20 reported LD; 8/20 (40%) signal-verified.** Simplified version of an earlier Stumbrys & Erlacher protocol — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1053810019303472); [PhilPapers](https://philpapers.org/rec/APPISL) **[V]**
- **Gott et al. 2020**, *Consciousness and Cognition* 84:102988. Four studies on sleep fragmentation; see Q1 §2 — [BORIS](https://boris.unibe.ch/189104) **[V]**
- **Erlacher, Furrer, Ineichen, Braillard & Schmid 2022**, *Clocks & Sleep* 4(2):230–239, https://doi.org/10.3390/clockssleep4020021. 4.5 h vs 6 h sleep before WBTB; **26% / 0% / 20%** vs 53% originally — [PMC9149976](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9149976/) **[V]**
- **Tan & Fan 2023** (online 2022), *Journal of Sleep Research* 32(3):e13786, https://doi.org/10.1111/jsr.13786, "A systematic review of new empirical data on lucid dream induction techniques". **19 peer-reviewed studies (2012–about 2021), 14 techniques.** **MILD most effective (6 studies).** SSILD "insufficient/ambiguous" (1 study). New taxonomy: cognitive techniques / external stimulation / substance intervention / cortical stimulation. It is a systematic review, **not a quantitative meta-analysis** as far as could be verified — [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jsr.13786) **[V]**
- **Klofat et al. 2024**, *Somnologie*, https://doi.org/10.1007/s11818-024-00453-8. WBTB alone at home: **4.3%** (N = 138), not above the spontaneous rate — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11818-024-00453-8) **[V]**
- **Konkoly, Whitmore, Mallett, Mazurek & Paller 2024**, *Consciousness and Cognition* 103759, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.concog.2024.103759, "Provoking lucid dreams at home with sensory cues paired with pre-sleep cognitive training". A smartphone app (no polysomnography) ran **~20 min nightly pre-sleep training** in which sounds cued a "lucid mindset" (critically analysing current experience). The cues were replayed during sleep (TLR = targeted lucidity reactivation). Exp 1: more LD during the TLR week than the prior week. Exp 2: more LD on TLR nights than on blinded control nights. Works best when the cues match the training sounds. Press coverage: the app roughly "**tripled**" lucid dreaming — [Northwestern PDF](https://faculty.wcas.northwestern.edu/paller/C&C24.pdf); [PsyPost](https://www.psypost.org/lucid-dreaming-app-triples-users-awareness-in-dreams-study-finds/); [MedicalXpress](https://medicalxpress.com/news/2024-10-lucid-proven-methods-unveiled-scientists.html) **[V; exact N and effect sizes not retrieved]**
- **Paller lab 2024**, IJoDR, "Lucid dreams from reactivating mindfulness during REM sleep" — [PDF](https://faculty.wcas.northwestern.edu/paller/IJDR2024.pdf) **[V existence only]**
- **Multi-centre preprint 2024** (bioRxiv 10.1101/2024.06.21.600133): SSILD + TLR cues + wearable EEG in naps. **51.7% (31/60)** signal-verified LD in the revised version — [bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.06.21.600133.full.pdf) **[V; preprint]**
- **Schredl 2025**, *IJoDR* 18(2):201–206, https://doi.org/10.11588/ijodr.2025.2.110523. RT vs autosuggestion, **N = 81**, 4 weeks, **both null**; prior LD frequency was the key predictor — [IJoDR](https://journals.ub.uni-heidelberg.de/index.php/IJoDR/article/view/110523) **[V]**
- **Schredl & Göritz 2025**, *Imagination, Cognition and Personality*, "Stability of Lucid Dream Frequency and Lucid Dreaming Skills Over a 7-Year Period". People who engaged with lucid dreaming over the 7 years showed a larger increase in LD frequency than those who did not. This suggests long-term practice works outside the lab (observational) — [SAGE, doi:10.1177/02762366251349518](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/02762366251349518) **[V]**
- **Peters, Erlacher et al. 2026 (preprint)**, bioRxiv 10.64898/2026.03.11.711049, "Waking Up in the Dream Lab". **3 studies, total N = 101.** Morning nap after **verbal LD instructions + pre-sleep audio priming recognition of the lab context.** **About 40–45% of dreams were externally rated lucid; 11–32% were signal-verified LDs in every group.** VR rehearsal, haptic stimulation in REM, and VR "glitches" did **not** improve on the baseline instruction procedure — [bioRxiv v2](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.03.11.711049v2.full) **[V; preprint]**
- **Dodet et al. 2015**, *Sleep* 38(3):487–497, "Lucid dreaming in narcolepsy", https://doi.org/10.5665/sleep.4516. Higher LD frequency in narcolepsy, consistent with sleep-onset REM / WILD mechanisms **[PK]**.
- **Meta-analysis status:** I found **no quantitative meta-analysis of LD induction efficacy** published 2023–2026. The only meta-analyses found are on prevalence (Saunders 2016). The 2012 and 2023 works are systematic reviews — [search result summary](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/365616489_A_systematic_review_of_new_empirical_data_on_lucid_dream_induction_techniques) **[V absence in searches; not proof of absence]**

### Inferences
- Even in the "best" field condition (RT+WBTB+MILD), success is about 1 in 6 nights for motivated volunteers. Lab nap protocols reach 40–50% because of explicit instructions, a well-timed REM awakening, a long REM-rich nap and demand effects. Peters et al. 2026 argue that the instructions and context matter more than the technology.
- A separate trend: single home techniques tested in isolation (WBTB alone 2024; RT alone and autosuggestion alone 2025) do not beat baseline. This supports **combination protocols**.

### Gaps
- Full-text effect sizes (odds ratios, Cohen's d) for ILDIS, Konkoly 2024 and Carr 2020 could not be retrieved.
- "Tan & Fan 2023 meta-analysis in *Neuroscience & Biobehavioral Reviews*": this does **not** exist as described. The Tan & Fan review is in the *Journal of Sleep Research*.

## Q3. How techniques are evolving; best combinations and mechanisms

### Takeaway
After 2015, the field moved from single cognitive techniques to **multi-component protocols**: WBTB timing, a pre-sleep cognitive technique (MILD or SSILD), and, in labs, sensory cues associated with a "lucid mindset" (TLR). New cognitive techniques in this period are SSILD (formally tested 2020) and cue-paired "lucid mindset" training (2020–2024). Proposed mechanisms: prospective memory, wake-REM proximity / REM pressure, and higher cortical arousal at sleep onset.

### Cited Findings
- **Components that add value:** MILD needs WBTB. RT+WBTB without MILD did not raise LD in 2017. Only the triple combination did — [PMC9149976 summary](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9149976/) **[V]**
- **Timing matters:** 6 h before interruption beat 4.5 h. REM-based morning awakenings were necessary in the lab — [Erlacher et al. 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9149976/) **[V]**
- **Sleep-onset speed after the technique** is a strong moderator: 46% success if asleep within 5 min (2017); "within 10 min" predicted success in ILDIS — [Adelaide](https://adelaide.edu.au/news/news95682.html); [ILDIS](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7379166/) **[V]**. *Interpretation (Aspy):* the intention has to stay active in prospective memory until REM starts. Falling asleep quickly also means the next REM period comes soon after rehearsal **[PK]**.
- **Wake↔REM transitions** are associated with LD — [Gott 2020](https://boris.unibe.ch/189104/1/1-s2.0-S1053810019305227-main.pdf) **[V]**. Neural rationale: LD shows more wake-like prefrontal activation — [Gott 2020 intro](https://boris.unibe.ch/189104) **[V]**; [Baird et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neubiorev.2019.03.008) **[PK]**
- **Cue-paired cognitive training** (TLR): train a "lucid mindset" while hearing sounds, then replay the sounds during REM or sleep. Lab: 50% signal-verified (Carr 2020). Home app: more LD than control nights (Konkoly 2024). With SSILD + EEG wearable: ~52% (2024 preprint) — sources in Q2 **[V]**. *(Sound and device delivery is covered by the other researcher. The cognitive half, a 20-min pre-sleep "lucid mindset" exercise linked to a distinctive sound, can be implemented in any app.)*
- **Instructions and context:** explicit LD instructions plus priming of the predictable sleep context gave about 40–45% lucidity ratings in naps, with no extra benefit from VR or haptics — [Peters et al. 2026 preprint](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.03.11.711049v2.full) **[V]**
- **Prior experience dominates outcome:** in a 2025 RCT, baseline LD frequency explained most of the outcome — [Schredl 2025](https://journals.ub.uni-heidelberg.de/index.php/IJoDR/article/view/110523) **[V]**. In the 2024 home WBTB trial, all 8 people who succeeded were already experienced — [Klofat 2024](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11818-024-00453-8) **[V]**
- **HCI/design research:** "LuciEntry: Towards Understanding the Design of Lucid Dream Induction" (ACM DIS 2025) is a design study of induction systems — [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3715336.3735790) **[V existence; content not retrieved]**

### Inferences
- **Evidence-supported "best" home stack for an app:**
  1. Daytime: RT about 10×/day + PM targets + dream journal.
  2. Bed: recall intention.
  3. Alarm about 5–6 h after sleep onset, ideally timed to the end of a ~90-min cycle (inference).
  4. Wake period: brief at home (Aspy) up to about 30–60 min in the lab. Recall and write down the dream.
  5. MILD (or SSILD), ending by settling quickly into sleep.
  6. On waking: DEILD-style stillness and recall.
  The weak links are getting back to sleep fast and adherence.
- **Wake-period tension:** a longer wake (60 min) did best in the lab, but fast sleep onset predicts success at home. An app could make the wake duration adjustable (e.g. 5–30 min) and track sleep-onset latency per user.

### Gaps
- No study has optimised WBTB wake duration systematically at home. The lab studies used 30–60 min (galantamine placebo ≥30 min [PK]; Erlacher 60 min best [V]).
- I found no controlled evidence on how many repetitions of the MILD phrase are needed. LaBerge says to repeat "until the intention is set".

## Q4. Learning curve and prevalence

### Takeaway
About **55%** of people have had at least one LD and about **23%** have them monthly or more. A motivated novice using RT+WBTB+MILD has about a 1-in-6 chance per practice night in the first week. Prior LD frequency is the main predictor of success, so true novices may need weeks. Lab protocols get 40–50% of novices lucid within 1–2 nights.

### Cited Findings
- **Prevalence (Saunders et al. 2016):** 34 studies (1966–2016): lifetime LD **55% (95% CI 49–62%)**. 25 studies: frequent LD (≥1/month) **23% (95% CI 20–25%)**. Quality-effects model; no moderators found — [NECTAR](https://nectar.northampton.ac.uk/id/eprint/8705/) **[V]**. *Conflict:* the Psi Encyclopedia describes the 23% as "weekly or more". The abstract says monthly — [Psi Encyclopedia](https://psi-encyclopedia.spr.ac.uk/sites/default/files/ebook/article/saunders_david-721.pdf) **[V]**
- **First week:** 17.4% of nights lucid with RT+WBTB+MILD (2017). ILDIS found similar rates for MILD and SSILD — [Adelaide](https://adelaide.edu.au/news/news95682.html); [ILDIS](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7379166/) **[V]**
- **Lab, 1–2 nights:** 40% of complete novices produced signal-verified LDs (Appel 2020) — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1053810019303472) **[V]**
- **4 weeks with a weak technique (RT or autosuggestion alone):** no significant gain — [Schredl 2025](https://journals.ub.uni-heidelberg.de/index.php/IJoDR/article/view/110523) **[V]**
- **Long-term (7 years):** people who engage with LD practice increase their LD frequency more — [Schredl & Göritz 2025](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/02762366251349518) **[V]**
- **WBTB alone at home in a one-night test:** success only among people with prior experience — [Klofat 2024](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11818-024-00453-8) **[V]**

### Inferences
- An app should set expectations of **weeks, not nights**, for most novices, while telling users that a first LD in week 1 is realistic for about 1 in 6 practice nights in motivated users. Since baseline LD frequency predicts outcomes, an onboarding questionnaire could personalise expectations.

### Gaps
- I found no well-controlled data on the median "nights to first LD" for true novices at home.

## Q5. Dream recall as a prerequisite

### Takeaway
Good dream recall is an empirical predictor of LD induction success (ILDIS). Recommended methods are journaling, a pre-sleep recall intention, waking slowly without distraction or motion, and waking from REM (later in the night). Most of this evidence is older and partly [PK].

### Cited Findings
- **Better general dream recall predicted successful induction** in ILDIS — [Aspy 2020](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7379166/) **[V]**
- ILDIS and the 2017 study used a **1-week baseline dream and sleep logbook**, which acts as recall training — [ILDIS](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7379166/); [Adelaide](https://adelaide.edu.au/news/news95682.html) **[V]**
- **Arousal–retrieval model and the interference effect** **[PK]**: dreams are recalled when the person wakes up enough to encode them. Distraction or activity right after waking reduces recall — Koulack & Goodenough 1976, *Psychological Bulletin* 83(5):975–984, https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.83.5.975. Cohen & Wolfe 1973 found that a distracting task right after waking reduced recall — *J Consult Clin Psychol* 41(3):349–355, https://doi.org/10.1037/h0035333 [PK]. *App implication:* lie still, keep eyes closed, rehearse the dream, then write it down; avoid screen notifications first.
- **High recallers have more intra-sleep wakefulness and greater brain reactivity to sounds** — Eichenlaub et al. 2014, *Neuropsychopharmacology* 39(7):1594–1602, https://doi.org/10.1038/npp.2014.6; Vallat et al. 2017, *Frontiers in Human Neuroscience* 11:132 **[PK]**. This supports the idea that brief awakenings (WBTB) aid recall.
- **Diaries increase reported recall** compared with questionnaire estimates. Keeping a diary itself raises recall frequency — Schredl 2002, *Dreaming* 12(1):17–26 ("Questionnaires and diaries as research instruments in dream research"), https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1013890421674 **[PK]**
- **Vitamin B6 (240 mg)** improved recall in one RCT (Aspy, Madden & Delfabbro 2018, *Perceptual and Motor Skills* 125(3):451–462, https://doi.org/10.1177/0031512518770326) **[PK]**. This is a supplement, so out of scope here; mention only.
- **Alarm-free waking:** I found no direct trials comparing alarm and alarm-free waking for recall. Recommendations to wake naturally come from LaBerge & Rheingold (1990) **[PK]**. REM awakenings are known to give the highest recall (classic lab finding) **[PK]**.

### Inferences
- App flow: a "recall first" module for 1–2 weeks (journal, recall intention, morning stillness ritual) before or alongside MILD. Recall improves and gives the user dream signs to target.

### Gaps
- Quantified effect sizes for journaling and recall intention as stand-alone interventions were not retrieved this session.

## Q6. Evidence grading per technique

### Takeaway
None of these techniques has strong evidence (multiple large preregistered RCTs). The strongest is WBTB + MILD (moderate). SSILD is limited. RT, autosuggestion and reflection are weak or null alone. Cue-paired "lucid mindset" training is promising but preliminary. WILD, FILD, DEILD and CAT are anecdotal.

### Cited Findings (grades assigned by this researcher from the sources above)
| Technique | Origin (year) | Best evidence | Grade |
|---|---|---|---|
| MILD (+WBTB) | LaBerge 1980 [PK] | 6 supporting studies (Tan & Fan 2023 [V]); 17.4% of nights field, ~46% if asleep <5 min (Aspy 2017 [V]); ~50% lab (Erlacher & Stumbrys 2020 [V]) | **Moderate** (controlled field + small lab; no large blinded RCT) |
| WBTB alone | LaBerge 1980s–94 [PK] | 4.3% (Klofat 2024 [V]); 18% vs 6% (summary only) | **Weak/null alone**; **essential component** |
| WBTB timing | Erlacher 2020/2022 [V] | 6 h > 4.5 h; REM-based awakenings | **Small controlled lab** |
| SSILD | CosmicIron, ~2010s [PK] | ILDIS ≈ MILD (N = 355) [V]; lab component 52% (preprint) [V] | **Limited** (1 field study) |
| Reality testing alone | Tholey 1983 / LaBerge 1990 [PK] | Null in Aspy 2017 [V] and Schredl 2025 [V]; < MILD in lab 2022 [V] | **Weak/null alone** |
| Specific checks (nose-pinch etc.) | Community/LaBerge [PK] | No comparative data | **Expert opinion** |
| Autosuggestion | Tholey 1983 [PK] | Null RCT 2025 [V] | **Weak/null** |
| Reflection–intention (Tholey) | Tholey 1983; Paulsson & Parker 2006 [PK] | Small effects, low quality (Stumbrys 2012 [V]) | **Small controlled, low quality** |
| Tholey combined | Tholey 1983 [PK] | Uncontrolled reports | **Anecdotal / uncontrolled** |
| Prospective memory training | LaBerge 1990 [PK] | Mechanism only | **Theoretical** |
| Dream journaling / dream signs | LaBerge 1985 [PK] | Recall predicts LD (ILDIS [V]) | **Indirect** |
| Cue-paired lucid-mindset training (TLR, cognitive part) | Carr 2020; Konkoly 2024 [V] | 50% lab; app > control nights | **Preliminary controlled** |
| Instruction + context priming in naps | Peters et al. 2026 [V] | 40–45% rated lucid, N = 101 | **Preprint** |
| WILD / hypnagogic | LaBerge 1985; Tholey 1983 [PK] | Mechanistic support (narcolepsy [PK], wake→REM [V]) | **Anecdotal** |
| FILD | Forums [PK] | none | **Anecdotal** |
| DEILD / dream chaining | LaBerge 1990; forums [PK] | none | **Anecdotal** |

### Inferences
- An app should label techniques with these grades ("research-backed", "promising", "community technique") and avoid claims like "54% success rate" seen on commercial blogs, such as [oneironauts.io](https://oneironauts.io/blog/mild-technique-complete-guide), which present figures without clear sourcing **[V that blogs make these claims]**.

### Gaps
- Tan & Fan's per-technique quality ratings were not retrieved (full text blocked).
- Exact protocols and outcomes for some newer items (Paller IJoDR 2024 mindfulness; LuciEntry 2025) were not retrieved.
- Search budget was exhausted before these could be verified: Stumbrys 2012 per-technique details, Tholey's reported success numbers, LaBerge 1994 NightLight numbers, Dodet 2015 numbers.
